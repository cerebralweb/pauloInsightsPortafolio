THE MIGRATION FEE HIDDEN INSIDE EVERY CLOUD ONBOARDING QUOTE FOR ELEVEN ENGINEERS

In this article ❧ Cloud vendors quote one figure per user, and that figure mixes charges of different kinds. A one time setup fee sits beside a recurring subscription, so the headline number tells a buyer nothing about what the service costs to keep. Separating a fixed charge from a recurring one, then reducing the recurring part to a single period, is the move that turns a quote into a rate a budget can carry.

The Problem

An analytics team of 11 engineers moved its workloads to a public cloud platform. The vendor charges 150,000 per engineer, which includes a one time data migration fee of 85,000 and two monthly bundles of compute and storage.

How much does each bundle cost?

  • a) 65,000
  • b) 32,500
  • c) 16,250
  • d) 15,000
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: b) 32,500 ✅

Math Solution

The quoted total per engineer holds the migration fee plus two bundles of equal price, so subtracting the fee leaves 65,000 for the recurring part, and dividing that remainder by two gives the price of one bundle.

Notation. T is the total quoted per engineer, 150,000. S is the migration fee inside that total, 85,000. p is the price of one bundle, the unknown. The governing equation is T = S + 2p.

The count of engineers never enters the calculation, because the quote is already stated per engineer.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Substitution: 150,000 = 85,000 + 2p
  2. Subtraction property of equality: 150,000 − 85,000 = (85,000 + 2p) − 85,000
  3. Commutativity and associativity of addition: (85,000 + 2p) − 85,000 = 2p + (85,000 − 85,000)
  4. Additive inverse and additive identity: 2p + (85,000 − 85,000) = 2p + 0 = 2p
  5. Distributivity of multiplication over subtraction: 150,000 − 85,000 = (1000)(150 − 85) = (1000)(65) = 65,000
  6. Transitivity of equality: 2p = 65,000
  7. Multiplication property of equality: (1/2)(2p) = (1/2)(65,000)
  8. Multiplicative inverse and multiplicative identity: (1/2)(2p) = (1)p = p
  9. Cancellation law: (1/2)(65,000) = 65,000/2 = (2)(32,500)/2 = 32,500
  10. Transitivity of equality: p = 32,500

Verification. 85,000 + 2(32,500) = 85,000 + 65,000 = 150,000, which matches the quoted total per engineer.

Historical Background

Separating a fixed charge from a recurring one is an old accounting habit. Venetian merchants of the fifteenth century already split the cost of a voyage into the outlay for the ship and the running charges for crew and provisions, because only the second figure told them what another month at sea would cost. Nineteenth century railways formalized the same split, since a line had to recover the capital spent on track once and the cost of running trains every day. Utility regulators later built tariffs on that logic, with a connection charge set apart from a consumption rate, and cloud vendors inherited the structure when they began billing setup fees alongside monthly subscriptions.
