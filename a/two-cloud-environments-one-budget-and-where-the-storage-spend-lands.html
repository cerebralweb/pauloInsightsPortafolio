TWO CLOUD ENVIRONMENTS, ONE BUDGET, AND WHERE THE STORAGE SPEND LANDS

In this article ❧ Cloud budgets rarely arrive itemized. A finance team is handed one total and a rule about how two spend lines relate to each other, and the allocation has to be recovered from that pair of facts before any account owner can be told what to plan for. This problem shows how a single budget total plus one stated gap between spend lines fixes the whole allocation, and how each inference in the recovery can be traced to a named property.

The Problem

A company runs two identical cloud environments, one for each of two teams. In each environment, compute spend is 12 thousand dollars more than storage spend. Total spend across both environments is 68 thousand dollars.

How much is storage spend in each environment?

  • a) 36 thousand dollars
  • b) 6.8 thousand dollars
  • c) 23 thousand dollars
  • d) 11 thousand dollars
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: d) 11 thousand dollars ✅

Math Solution

Each environment holds two spend lines, so the 12 thousand dollar gap is counted twice inside the 68 thousand dollar total. Removing 24 from 68 leaves the part of the budget split evenly across four equal spend blocks, and dividing 44 by 4 fixes storage spend per environment.

The stated gap belongs to one environment, the budget belongs to two, so the gap enters the total at double its size.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Spend per environment: storage plus storage plus 12
  2. Combined spend: 2(2s + 12) = 68
  3. Expanded total: 4s + 24 = 68
  4. Gap removed: 68 − 24 = 44, so 4s = 44
  5. Even split: 44 / 4 = 11, so s = 11
  6. Companion line: compute spend is 11 + 12 = 23
  7. Conclusion: storage spend is 11 thousand dollars in each environment

The Solution With Every Property Named

Mode. Compute.

Setup. Let s be the storage spend in one environment and c the compute spend in one environment, both in thousands of dollars. Compute spend exceeds storage spend by 12, so c = s + 12. Each environment spends s + c, and the two environments are identical, so combined spend is 2(s + c). The budget fixes combined spend at 68. So 2(s + c) = 68.

Notation.

  • s is the storage spend per environment in thousands of dollars, the unknown, a positive rational.
  • c is the compute spend per environment in thousands of dollars, 23 once Step 8 fixes s.
  • 12 is the amount by which compute spend exceeds storage spend in one environment.
  • 2 is the number of environments.
  • 68 is the combined spend across both environments.
  • 1/4 is the multiplicative inverse of 4.
  • ⇒ reads implies.
  • ≠ reads is not equal to.

Step 1 — Substitution

Property. Substitution. x = y ⇒ x may replace y.

Inference. 2(s + c) = 68 and c = s + 12, so 2(s + (s + 12)) = 68.

Step 2 — Associativity of addition

Property. Associativity of addition. x + (y + z) = (x + y) + z.

Inference. 2((s + s) + 12) = 68. From line 1.

Step 3 — Multiplicative identity and distributivity

Property. Multiplicative identity and distributivity. (1)x = x and xz + yz = (x + y)z.

Inference. s + s = (1)s + (1)s = (1 + 1)s = 2s, so 2(2s + 12) = 68. From line 2.

Step 4 — Distributivity of multiplication over addition

Property. Distributivity. z(x + y) = zx + zy.

Inference. 2(2s + 12) = (2)(2s) + (2)(12) = 4s + 24, so 4s + 24 = 68. From line 3.

Step 5 — Subtraction property of equality

Property. Subtraction property of equality. x = y ⇒ x − z = y − z.

Inference. (4s + 24) − 24 = 68 − 24 ⇒ 4s = 44. From line 4.

Step 6 — Division property of equality

Property. Division property of equality. x = y and z ≠ 0 ⇒ x/z = y/z.

Inference. 4s/4 = 44/4. From line 5.

Step 7 — Cancellation law

Property. Cancellation law. For y ≠ 0, (yx)/y = x.

Inference. 4s/4 = s and 44/4 = (4)(11)/4 = 11.

Step 8 — Transitivity of equality

Property. Transitivity of equality. x = y and y = z ⇒ x = z.

Inference. s = 11. From lines 6 and 7.

Verification. c = 11 + 12 = 23, and 2(11 + 23) = 2(34) = 68. Both sides match.

Answer. s = 11. Storage spend is 11 thousand dollars in each environment, option d.

Historical Background

Problems of this shape, a known total and a stated gap between shares, were the working material of accountants long before they were teaching examples. Medieval Islamic scholarship in Baghdad organized them into a method for restoring and balancing an unknown quantity, and the treatises that carried it circulated because merchants, tax collectors, and estate administrators needed shares recovered from totals every day. The modern cloud invoice is a late entry in that lineage, since a finance team still receives one figure for the whole and a rule about how the parts relate.

Why 1 Is Called the Identity, in Plain English

Step 3 leans on a property that looks like it says nothing. Two characters argue it out.

Dustin. Multiply any number by 1 and it walks away unharmed. That's the whole trick.

Steve. So the number stays itself. Fine. Why do we call that an identity.

Dustin. Because of who did the not harming. The name goes to 1, not to the number.

Steve. Wait. The number keeps its identity, but 1 gets the credit.

Dustin. 1 is the one doing the work. Or the one doing no work, which is the work.

Steve. That's the laziest job in mathematics.

Dustin. It's the most reliable. It has to hold for every number. Not most of them. All of them.

Steve. And if it only worked on, what, sevens.

Dustin. Then 1 gets demoted. No title.

Steve. Harsh. Is there another one of these.

Dustin. Addition has 0. Add 0 to anything and nothing happens.

Steve. Two numbers with the same non job.

Dustin. Different operations. And each operation gets exactly one.

Steve. Says who.

Dustin. Says the math. Suppose two numbers, e and f, both did nothing. Then e equals e times f, and e times f equals f. So e equals f. They were the same number pretending to be twins.

Steve. Okay, that was slightly cool.

Dustin. There's a second meaning too. An identity is any equation true for every value you throw at it. So the sentence 1 times x equals x is itself an identity.

Steve. The identity is about the identity.

Dustin. And there's a function that does the same thing. Feed it anything, get it back.

Steve. So the function is unemployed as well.

Dustin. Everyone in this family is unemployed. That's the family business.
