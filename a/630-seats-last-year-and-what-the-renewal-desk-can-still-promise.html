630 SEATS LAST YEAR, AND WHAT THE RENEWAL DESK CAN STILL PROMISE

In this article ❧ Renewal figures arrive as survivors. A vendor sees what stayed, not what left, and every account review starts by rebuilding the base that produced the current count. This problem shows how a reverse share recovers a prior year from a surviving one, and how two different mental routes reach the same figure under different properties.

The Problem

The account team at Robert Crook Software is concerned because GenAI assistant seat licenses fell 20 % compared with last year. This year 504 seats were renewed.

How many seats were renewed last year?

  • a) 63
  • b) 2520
  • c) 630
  • d) 540
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: c) 630 ✅

Math Solution

The 504 renewed seats stand for the retained four fifths, so the base is recovered by dividing 504 by 4 to size one fifth and then adding that fifth back. One fifth is 126, and 504 plus 126 is 630.

The divisor comes from the part kept, not the part lost, which is why 504 splits into 4 parts and not 5.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Retained share: 100 % minus 20 % leaves 80 %, which is 4/5
  2. Reading of 504: 504 stands for (4/5)N, so 504 holds four equal fifths
  3. Size of one fifth: 504 divided by 4 gives 126
  4. Rebuild of the base: N equals the four fifths kept plus the fifth lost
  5. Last year: 504 plus 126 gives 630
  6. Check: four fifths of 630 returns 504, and 20 % of 630 is 126

The Fifth That Went Missing

The first route treats the retained share as four copies of a single unit. Every step names the property that licenses it.

Setup. Let N be the seats renewed last year. A decline of 20 % leaves four fifths standing, and those four fifths are the 504 seats renewed this year. So (4/5)N = 504.

Notation. N is last year's seats, the unknown, a positive integer. 504 is this year's seats. 4/5 is the retained share. 1/5 is the lost share, and also one fifth of N. 1/4 is the multiplicative inverse of 4. 630 is the value of N. The symbol ⇒ reads implies, and ≠ reads is not equal to.

  1. Definition of division. x/y = x(1/y) for y ≠ 0, so 4/5 = (4)(1/5).
  2. Substitution. (4)(1/5)N = 504.
  3. Associativity of multiplication. (xy)z = x(yz), so 4((1/5)N) = 504.
  4. Multiplication property of equality. x = y ⇒ cx = cy, so (1/4)(4)((1/5)N) = (1/4)(504).
  5. Multiplicative inverse and identity. (1/4)(4) = 1 and 1x = x, so the left side reduces to (1/5)N.
  6. Cancellation law. (yx)/y = x for y ≠ 0, so (1/4)(504) = (4)(126)/4 = 126.
  7. Transitivity of equality. (1/5)N = 126.
  8. Addition of fractions with a common denominator. 4/5 + 1/5 = 5/5 = 1.
  9. Multiplicative identity. N = (1)N = (4/5 + 1/5)N.
  10. Distributivity. (x + y)z = xz + yz, so (4/5 + 1/5)N = (4/5)N + (1/5)N.
  11. Substitution. N = 504 + 126.
  12. Transitivity of equality. N = 630.

Verification. (4/5)(630) = (4)(630)/5 = 2520/5 = 504. Both sides match. The two steps carrying the route are step 4, which takes a quarter of both sides because four fifths is four copies of one fifth, and step 10, which rebuilds N as the share kept plus the share lost.

Borrowing the Structure of 80

The second route measures the unit on the percent scale first, then reads the same structure on the count. It also splits 504 into two friendly parts.

Setup. Let u be one fifth of N. A decline of 20 % keeps 80 %, and 80 % is four fifths, so the seats kept are 4u and 4u = 504, while N = 5u.

Notation. u is one fifth of N. 80 is the retained percent and 20 the lost percent. 500 and 4 are the two parts of 504. The remaining symbols carry the meaning given in the first route.

  1. Definition of percent. x % = x/100, so 80 % = 80/100.
  2. Cancellation law. (yx)/(yz) = x/z, so 80/100 = (20)(4)/((20)(5)) = 4/5.
  3. Cancellation law, again. 80/4 = (4)(20)/4 = 20, so one fifth reads 20 on the percent scale and the retained share holds 4 equal parts.
  4. Multiplication property of equality. (1/4)(4u) = (1/4)(504).
  5. Multiplicative inverse and identity. u = 504/4.
  6. Division distributes over addition. (x + y)/z = x/z + y/z for z ≠ 0, so 504/4 = 500/4 + 4/4.
  7. Cancellation law, on the large part. 500/4 = (4)(125)/4 = 125.
  8. Multiplicative inverse. 4/4 = 1.
  9. Substitution. u = 125 + 1 = 126.
  10. Distributivity. N = 5u = (4 + 1)u = 4u + u.
  11. Substitution. N = 504 + 126 = 630.

Verification. (4)(126) = 504, matching the seats kept, and (5)(126) = 630. One caution on the friendly split. 500 divided by 4 is 125, not 124. With 124 the unit would read 125 and the base 629, and four fifths of 629 is not an integer, so the check fails at once.

Historical Background

Merchants in Renaissance Italy already worked backwards from a surviving amount to an original one. Tax collection, weight loss in transported grain, and interest deducted at the point of sale all produced a figure that stood for a share rather than a whole, and the commercial arithmetic manuals of the period drilled the recovery of the base as a standing exercise. The same operation runs through modern renewal analysis, churn reporting, and inflation adjusted revenue, where the number on the page is the remainder and the question is what it came from.
