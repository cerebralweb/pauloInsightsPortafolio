FOUR GENAI LICENSES IN ONE ORDER, OR FOUR SEPARATE INVOICES

In this article ❧ Vendors rarely publish the price a buyer actually pays. Credits, rebates and pack terms sit between the list price and the invoice, and a team that reads only the invoice never learns what an order habit costs it over a year of renewals. This problem shows how a single order credit hides two different unit prices, and how a short script turns the comparison into a reusable procurement check.

The Problem

A startup buys GenAI assistant licenses for its analysts. When the team buys 4 licenses in a single order, the vendor applies a 10 dollar credit and the invoice comes to 44 dollars.

How much more does the team pay per license if it buys the licenses one at a time instead of all in one order?

  • a) 2.5 dollars
  • b) 4 dollars
  • c) 2 dollars
  • d) 5 dollars
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: a) 2.5 dollars ✅

Math Solution

The credit lands once on the whole order, so adding it back to the invoice recovers the undiscounted order price, and dividing that price by 4 gives the price of a license bought alone. Dividing the invoice by 4 gives the bundled price, and the difference between the two is the premium paid for ordering one at a time.

A flat credit spread over 4 units is worth 2.5 dollars per unit, so the whole answer is the credit divided by the pack size.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Order price before the credit: 44 + 10 = 54 dollars
  2. Price of one license bought alone: 54 / 4 = 13.5 dollars
  3. Price per license inside the order: 44 / 4 = 11 dollars
  4. Premium per license: 13.5 − 11 = 2.5 dollars
  5. Check: 4 × 13.5 − 10 = 44 dollars, which matches the invoice
  6. Conclusion: buying one at a time costs 2.5 dollars more per license, or 10 dollars across the 4 licenses

Property by Property Solution

The breakdown above gives the arithmetic. The sequence below gives the licence for each move, one named property per step, so every line can be checked without trusting the previous one.

Setup

A single order of 4 licenses invoices 44 dollars after a 10 dollar credit. Let p be the price of one license bought on its own, q the price per license inside the bundled order, and d the extra amount paid per license when the licenses are bought one at a time. The credit reduces the full order price to the invoice, so 4p − 10 = 44. The bundled unit price divides the invoice across the 4 licenses, so 4q = 44. The question asks for d = p − q.

Notation

  • p is the price of one license bought on its own, in dollars, the unknown of Step 6.
  • q is the price per license inside the bundled order, in dollars, the unknown of Step 7.
  • d is the extra amount paid per license outside the bundle, in dollars, the unknown the problem asks for.
  • 4 is the number of licenses in the order.
  • 10 is the credit the vendor applies once to the order, in dollars.
  • 44 is the invoiced amount after the credit, in dollars.
  • 1/4 is the multiplicative inverse of 4.
  • 1/2 is the multiplicative inverse of 2.
  • c, x, y, z are any numbers, used to state a property in general form.
  • ⇒ reads implies.
  • ≠ reads is not equal to.

Step 1 — Addition property of equality

Property. Addition property of equality. x = y ⇒ x + c = y + c.
Inference. (4p − 10) + 10 = 44 + 10. From Setup.

Step 2 — Additive inverse and additive identity

Property. Additive inverse and additive identity. (−c) + c = 0 and x + 0 = x.
Inference. 4p = 54. From line 1.

Step 3 — Multiplication property of equality

Property. Multiplication property of equality. x = y ⇒ cx = cy.
Inference. (1/4)(4p) = (1/4)(54). From line 2.

Step 4 — Multiplicative inverse and multiplicative identity

Property. Multiplicative inverse and multiplicative identity. For x ≠ 0, (1/x)x = 1 and 1x = x.
Inference. (1/4)(4p) = (1)p = p. From line 3.

Step 5 — Cancellation law

Property. Cancellation law. For y ≠ 0 and z ≠ 0, (yx)/(yz) = x/z.
Inference. (1/4)(54) = 54/4 = (2)(27)/((2)(2)) = 27/2.

Step 6 — Transitivity of equality

Property. Transitivity of equality. x = y and y = z ⇒ x = z.
Inference. p = 27/2. From lines 4 and 5.

Step 7 — Cancellation law, as in Step 5

Property. Cancellation law, as in Step 5.
Inference. 4q = 44 ⇒ q = 44/4 = (4)(11)/4 = 11. From Setup.

Step 8 — Substitution

Property. Substitution. x = y ⇒ x may replace y.
Inference. d = 27/2 − 11. From lines 6 and 7.

Step 9 — Distributivity of multiplication over addition

Property. Distributivity of multiplication over addition. x(y − z) = xy − xz.
Inference. 27/2 − 11 = (1/2)(27) − (1/2)(22) = (1/2)(27 − 22) = (1/2)(5) = 5/2. From line 8.

Verification

4(27/2) − 10 = 54 − 10 = 44. The invoice matches.
q + d = 11 + 5/2 = 22/2 + 5/2 = 27/2 = p. The unit prices match.

Answer

d = 5/2 = 2.5. Buying one license at a time costs 2.5 dollars more per license than buying the pack of 4, which is option a.

Historical Background

Comparing a bulk price with a single unit price is one of the oldest recorded uses of commercial arithmetic. The abacus schools of thirteenth century Italy trained merchant sons on exactly this family of questions, and Leonardo of Pisa filled the Liber Abaci of 1202 with problems on shared costs, barter terms and the price of a part once the price of a whole is known.

The same reasoning became routine in the wholesale trade of early modern Europe, where a rebate granted on a full consignment had to be traced back to the piece before a factor could quote a retail price. Modern software procurement inherited the practice with new vocabulary, since a pack credit, a committed use discount and a reserved instance rebate all pose the merchant's original question of what one unit truly costs.

Python Version

The script recovers both unit prices from an invoice and a flat order credit, so any pack offer can be tested with three inputs and no spreadsheet.

def order_premium(units, invoice, credit):
    """Compare the standalone unit price against the bundled unit price.

    units   number of items in the single order
    invoice amount billed after the credit
    credit  flat credit applied once to the whole order
    """
    full_order_price = invoice + credit
    standalone_price = full_order_price / units
    bundled_price = invoice / units
    premium = standalone_price - bundled_price
    return {
        "full_order_price": full_order_price,
        "standalone_price": standalone_price,
        "bundled_price": bundled_price,
        "premium_per_unit": premium,
        "premium_total": premium * units,
    }


if __name__ == "__main__":
    result = order_premium(units=4, invoice=44, credit=10)
    for key, value in result.items():
        print(f"{key} = {value}")

Run it with python order_premium.py and it returns a premium of 2.5 dollars per license and 10 dollars across the order.
