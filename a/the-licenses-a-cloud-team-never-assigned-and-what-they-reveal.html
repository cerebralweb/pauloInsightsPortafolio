THE LICENSES A CLOUD TEAM NEVER ASSIGNED, AND WHAT THEY REVEAL

In this article ❧ Capacity rarely stays where it was bought. A share goes into a reserved commitment, another share reaches the teams that asked for it, and what nobody claimed sits in a pool that finance still paid for. That small unclaimed remainder is often the only number on record, and it is enough to recover the size of the original order.

The Problem

A company ordered a batch of virtual machine licenses for the year. It placed 75 percent of the licenses into a reserved pool in the primary region and assigned 80 percent of the remaining licenses to business units from the shared pool. 25 licenses in the shared pool are still unassigned.

How many licenses were ordered originally?

  • a) 250
  • b) 600
  • c) 400
  • d) 500
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: d) 500 ✅

Math Solution

The shared pool is one quarter of the order, and what stays unassigned is one fifth of that shared pool, so the 25 licenses are one twentieth of the order. Multiplying 25 by 20 recovers the full batch.

A fraction of a fraction multiplies, which is why two clean cuts of 4 and 5 produce 20 equal parts instead of 9.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Shared pool share: 25% = 25/100 = 1/4 of the order
  2. Assigned share of that pool: 80% = 80/100 = 4/5
  3. Partition of that pool: 4/5 + u = 1, assigned and unassigned only
  4. Unassigned share of that pool: u = 5/5 − 4/5 = 1/5
  5. Unassigned share of the order: (1/5)(1/4) = 1/20
  6. Equation in one unknown: (1/20)T = 25
  7. Inverse step: (20)((1/20)T) = (20)(25)
  8. Order size: T = 500 licenses
  9. Check: reserved 375, shared 125, assigned 100, unassigned 25

From School Exercise to Licensing Decision

The source exercise counted notebooks stored in a warehouse and sold in a school store. The numbers, the relationships and the answer logic stay untouched. Only the actors change, from notebooks to virtual machine licenses, from a warehouse to a reserved pool in the primary region, from a school store to a shared pool drawn down by business units.

Candidate business topics were ranked by how naturally the structure fits, from a fixed batch that splits into a reserved commitment and an assignable remainder, down to topics where the second percentage reads awkwardly.

  1. IaaS capacity reservation: a fixed batch of provisioned units splits cleanly into a reserved pool and an assignable remainder
  2. Cloud cost management: committed spend units are parked in a discount plan first, then drawn down
  3. Storage: allocated volumes split into archive tiers and active tiers with a leftover balance
  4. MLOps: a batch of GPU job slots divides between scheduled training runs and ad hoc requests
  5. GenAI token credits: a purchased credit block reserves most capacity and releases the rest to users
  6. Serverless: a concurrency quota partitions across environments, though the units feel less countable
  7. Computer vision: annotation batches split across pipelines, yet the second percentage reads awkwardly as a sale

The mathematics topic is a percentage of a remainder, solved by working backward to a total. The quantities are the reserved pool at 75 percent of the order, the shared pool as the remaining 25 percent, the assigned share of that pool at 80 percent, the 25 licenses left unassigned, and the order quantity as the unknown.

The Proof, Property by Property

Compute mode. Let T be the licenses ordered. The reserved pool takes 75 percent of T. The shared pool keeps the remaining 25 percent of T. Of that shared pool, 80 percent is assigned to business units, and the pool holds assigned and unassigned licenses only. What stays unassigned is 25 licenses. Find T.

Notation

  • T is the licenses ordered, the unknown, a positive integer.
  • % reads per hundred, so p% = p/100.
  • 25% is the share of T kept in the shared pool.
  • 80% is the share of the shared pool that is assigned.
  • 25 is the licenses still unassigned in the shared pool.
  • (1/4)T is one quarter of T, the shared pool.
  • u is the unassigned share of the shared pool, produced in Step 5, a fraction between 0 and 1.
  • 1 stands for the shared pool taken as one whole, used in Step 5.
  • c is any number subtracted from both sides, used in Step 6.
  • 1/20 is the share of T that stays unassigned, produced in Step 11.
  • ⇒ reads implies.

Solution

  1. Definition of percent. p% = p/100. Inference. 25% = 25/100.
  2. Cancellation law. For y ≠ 0, (yx)/y = x. Inference. 25/100 = (25)(1)/(25)(4) = 1/4, so the shared pool is (1/4)T. From line 1.
  3. Definition of percent, as in Step 1. p% = p/100. Inference. 80% = 80/100.
  4. Cancellation law, as in Step 2. For y ≠ 0, (yx)/y = x. Inference. 80/100 = (20)(4)/(20)(5) = 4/5. From line 3.
  5. FLAG, partition assumption. No standard property licenses this move. It rests on the stated assumption that the shared pool holds assigned and unassigned licenses only. Inference. 4/5 + u = 1. From line 4.
  6. Subtraction property of equality. x = y ⇒ x − c = y − c. Inference. (4/5 + u) − 4/5 = 1 − 4/5. From line 5.
  7. Additive inverse and additive identity. x + (−x) = 0 and x + 0 = x. Inference. (4/5 + u) − 4/5 = u + 0 = u. From line 6.
  8. Multiplicative identity and the difference rule for fractions. 1x = x, and a/c − b/c = (a − b)/c. Inference. 1 − 4/5 = 5/5 − 4/5 = 1/5, so u = 1/5. From lines 6 and 7.
  9. Definition of a fraction of a quantity. (a/b) of x = (a/b)x. Inference. (1/5)((1/4)T) = 25. From lines 2 and 8.
  10. Associativity of multiplication. (xy)z = x(yz). Inference. (1/5)((1/4)T) = ((1/5)(1/4))T. From line 9.
  11. Product rule for fractions. (a/b)(c/d) = (ac)/(bd). Inference. (1/5)(1/4) = 1/20 ⇒ (1/20)T = 25. From lines 9 and 10.
  12. Multiplication property of equality. x = y ⇒ cx = cy. Inference. (20)((1/20)T) = (20)(25). From line 11.
  13. Multiplicative inverse and multiplicative identity. For x ≠ 0, (1/x)x = 1 and 1x = x. Inference. T = 500. From line 12.

Verification

(1/4)(500) = 125. (4/5)(125) = 100. 125 − 100 = 25. Both sides match.

Answer

T = 500. The company ordered 500 licenses.

The Original Sheet

The dotted verticals came from the cancellation law, since 25/100 reduces to 1/4. The five slices inside the last division came from the additive inverse, since what is left after 4/5 is 1/5. The two crossing cuts, four one way and five the other, are the product rule for fractions, which is why 20 cells appear and one of them is 1/20. Writing 500L under the whole square was the multiplication property of equality.

Historical Background

Working backward from a leftover is older than the percent sign. Scribes in ancient Egypt solved quantity puzzles by the method later called false position, guessing a whole, following it through the cuts, then scaling the guess by the ratio between the result and the figure on record. Merchant manuscripts in fifteenth century Italy pushed the same habit into commerce, where the phrase per cento became a standing way to express a share of a cargo, a tax or a profit. Both traditions answer the question a finance team asks about a cloud commitment, namely what the whole must have been for this remainder to appear.
