846,000 CREDITS, ONE ROUNDING RULE, EIGHT WEEKS TO TARGET

In this article ❧ Prepaid commitments reward teams that fund them on time and penalize teams that miss the threshold. When credits arrive in fixed weekly blocks, planners cannot lean on a partial week, so the leftover gap always pushes the deadline out by one more full cycle. This problem shows how subtraction, division, and rounding up combine into a small planning rule, and how Python turns that rule into a reusable tool.

The Problem

A cloud operations team is building up prepaid credits to lock in a reserved compute plan that costs 1,200,000 credits. Their finance department granted them a starting balance of 450,000 credits, and each week the team's budget adds 120,000 credits to the balance. This week they consumed 96,000 credits on a one off migration job.

How many weeks of accrual will the team need to reach 1,200,000 credits?

  • a) 5
  • b) 6
  • c) 2
  • d) 8
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: d) 8 ✅

Math Solution

First subtract the one off migration cost from the starting balance to get the true starting point. Then divide the remaining gap by the weekly accrual and round up, because a partial week never clears the threshold.

The gap of 846,000 credits divided by 120,000 per week gives 7.05, and any remainder forces one extra full week.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Balance after the one off cost: 450,000 − 96,000 → 354,000 credits
  2. Remaining gap to target: 1,200,000 − 354,000 → 846,000 credits
  3. Raw weeks needed: 846,000 ÷ 120,000 → 7.05 weeks
  4. Round up to whole weeks: 7.05 → 8 weeks
  5. Conclusion: The team needs 8 full weeks of accrual to reach the 1,200,000 credit target.

Historical Background

The habit of rounding a remainder up to the next whole unit is far older than the bracket notation for it. Ancient tax collectors and grain measurers already faced the same choice whenever a division left something over, since a fraction of a sack or a fraction of a workday had to resolve into a whole count for the record. The modern symbols ⌈ ⌉ for this operation arrived only in 1962, when Kenneth Iverson introduced them while designing the language that became APL, giving a clean written form to a rule merchants and administrators had used for millennia.

Python Version

This script strips out a one off cost, measures the gap to a target, and returns the number of full accrual periods needed, so it fits any prepaid budget question with the same shape.

import math

def weeks_to_target(target, start_balance, weekly_accrual, one_off_cost):
    """Return the whole weeks needed to reach target after a one off cost."""
    available = start_balance - one_off_cost
    gap = target - available
    if gap <= 0:
        return 0
    return math.ceil(gap / weekly_accrual)

weeks = weeks_to_target(1_200_000, 450_000, 120_000, 96_000)
print(weeks)

Run it with any interpreter and it prints 8, the number of full weeks the team needs to reach the target.
