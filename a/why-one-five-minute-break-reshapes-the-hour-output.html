WHY ONE FIVE MINUTE BREAK RESHAPES THE HOUR'S OUTPUT

In this article ❧ Capacity planning starts with a simple question. How much can a team produce in a fixed window? When several people work at once, their output stacks, but paid time and productive time are not the same thing. This problem shows how combined work rates and one removed break decide the true output of an hour, and how Python turns that logic into a reusable tool.

The Problem

At a shared services center, three clerks enter invoice records into the system. Julia enters 9 records per minute. Manuel works 4 times faster than Julia. Carlos enters half as many records per minute as Manuel. At the end of every hour, all three stop for a 5 minute break.

How many records do the three clerks enter in total between 10:00 a.m. and 11:30 a.m.?

  • a) 3765
  • b) 3,680
  • c) 5,355
  • d) 7,560
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: c) 5,355 ✅

Math Solution

Find each rate, then add the three into one team rate. Count the window as 90 minutes, subtract the single break, and multiply the team rate by the working minutes.

The three clerks enter 63 records per minute together, so every removed minute costs 63 records.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Manuel's rate: 4 × 9 = 36 records per minute
  2. Carlos's rate: 36 ÷ 2 = 18 records per minute
  3. Team rate: 9 + 36 + 18 = 63 records per minute
  4. Elapsed time: 10:00 to 11:30 = 90 minutes
  5. Break removed: one break of 5 minutes at the 11:00 boundary
  6. Working minutes: 90 − 5 = 85 minutes
  7. Total records: 63 × 85 = 5,355

Formal Proof

The same result, built as a chain where each step names the property that licenses it.

Setup. Let J be Julia's rate, 9 records per minute. Let M be Manuel's rate, defined as 4 times J. Let C be Carlos's rate, defined as half of M. Let T be the team rate, T = J + M + C. The window runs 90 minutes. Each break costs 5 minutes. Let W be the working minutes and N the total records, N = T × W.

Step 1 — FLAG, inspection

Property. None standard. This move rests on inspection of the clock window.

Inference. One break falls inside the window. The only hour boundary strictly inside 10:00 to 11:30 is 11:00, so exactly one 5 minute break occurs.

Step 2 — Substitution

Property. Substitution. Equals may replace equals.

Inference. M = 36. In M = 4 × J, replace J with 9, so M = 4 × 9 = 36.

Step 3 — Substitution

Property. Substitution, as in Step 2.

Inference. C = 18. In C = M ÷ 2, replace M with 36, so C = 36 ÷ 2 = 18. Builds on Step 2.

Step 4 — Associativity of addition, with substitution

Property. Associativity of addition. The grouping of a sum does not change its value.

Inference. T = 63. Replace the rates in J + M + C with 9, 36, 18, then group 9 + 36 first, giving 45, then 45 + 18 = 63. Builds on Steps 2 and 3.

Step 5 — Substitution

Property. Substitution, as in Step 2.

Inference. W = 85. Working minutes equal 90 minus the one break of 5 minutes, so 90 − 5 = 85. Builds on Step 1.

Step 6 — Distributive law, with substitution

Property. Distributive law. a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c.

Inference. N = 5355. Replace T and W with 63 and 85, then split 85 as 80 + 5. So 63 × 85 = 63 × 80 + 63 × 5 = 5040 + 315 = 5355. Builds on Steps 4 and 5.

Verification. 63 × 85 = 5355. Splitting the check, 63 × 80 = 5040 and 63 × 5 = 315, and 5040 + 315 = 5355. The value matches option c.

Historical Background

Adding the output of many workers to plan a fixed span is old work. State granaries and building sites in the ancient world paid scribes to record how many laborers worked and for how long, so overseers could forecast a day's total before the day ended.

The idea grew sharper in the early 20th century, when factory time studies measured output per worker per minute and summed it across a line. Managers learned the same lesson this problem teaches. Idle minutes, including scheduled rest, must come out of the count before any forecast holds.

Python Version

The script solves the same type of problem for any rates, any window, and any break pattern, so it works as a small throughput calculator.

# What this does: works out how many records three clerks enter in a time window.
# Where it came from: three clerks enter invoices at different speeds and take breaks.
# The math idea: add their rates, multiply by working minutes, ignore break time.
# You type in: Julia's rate, Manuel's speed factor, Carlos's fraction, total minutes, break minutes, and how many breaks.
# It prints: the total number of records the three clerks enter.

# This function does the math.
def total_records(julia_rate, manuel_factor, carlos_fraction, total_minutes, break_minutes, breaks):
    # Manuel works a set number of times faster than Julia.
    manuel_rate = julia_rate * manuel_factor
    # Carlos works a fraction of Manuel's rate.
    carlos_rate = manuel_rate * carlos_fraction
    # Add the three rates to get one team rate per minute.
    team_rate = julia_rate + manuel_rate + carlos_rate
    # Take out the minutes lost to breaks.
    working_minutes = total_minutes - (break_minutes * breaks)
    # Records equal team rate times the minutes actually worked.
    return team_rate * working_minutes

# Tell the reader what we will figure out.
print("Let's find how many records the three clerks enter.")

# Ask for Julia's records per minute.
julia_rate = float(input("Julia's records per minute: "))

# Ask how many times faster Manuel is than Julia.
manuel_factor = float(input("Manuel's speed factor versus Julia: "))

# Ask what fraction of Manuel's rate Carlos works at.
carlos_fraction = float(input("Carlos's fraction of Manuel's rate: "))

# Ask for the total minutes in the window.
total_minutes = float(input("Total minutes in the window: "))

# Ask how many minutes each break lasts.
break_minutes = float(input("Minutes per break: "))

# Ask how many breaks fall inside the window.
breaks = float(input("Number of breaks: "))

# Do the math.
result = total_records(julia_rate, manuel_factor, carlos_fraction, total_minutes, break_minutes, breaks)

# Show as a whole number when it has no leftover.
result = int(result) if result.is_integer() else result

# Print the answer with words that fit the scenario.
print("Total records entered by the three clerks:", result)

Run it in a terminal, type 9, 4, 0.5, 90, 5, and 1 at the prompts, and it returns 5355.
