288 SQUARE METERS OF SERVER HALL, AND THE DRAWING THAT PLANS IT

In this article ❧ Every facility decision starts on paper long before it starts on concrete. A floor plan carries space, cost, and capacity at once, so the factor that shrinks the building onto the page has to be stated without ambiguity. This problem shows how the ratio between two areas relates to the ratio between their sides, and why reading the first as the second misprices a plan.

The Problem

A cloud provider is planning a new server hall with 288 square meters of floor space. The facilities analyst draws the hall to scale. Her drawing measures 8 units long by 4 units high.

What scale did she use for each side of the hall?

  • a) 1/9
  • b) 1/3
  • c) 9
  • d) 3
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: b) 1/3 ✅

Math Solution

The drawing covers 32 square units, so the two areas stand in the ratio 32 to 288, which reduces to 1/9. That figure compares areas, and each area is a product of two sides, so the scale of one side is the square root of 1/9.

An area shrinks by the square of the factor applied to its sides, so 1/9 on the floor means 1/3 on the wall.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Drawn area: (8)(4) = 32 square units
  2. Actual area: 288 square meters
  3. Area ratio: 32/288 = 1/9
  4. Relation between the ratios: the area ratio equals the square of the side ratio
  5. Scale of one side: √(1/9) = 1/3
  6. Check: 8/(1/3) = 24, 4/(1/3) = 12, and (24)(12) = 288

Historical Background

Reduced plans are older than the drafting table. Roman surveyors laid out camps and aqueduct routes from measured sketches, and their manuals treated the reduction factor as part of the record rather than a convenience. Renaissance builders made the practice explicit, since a dome or a palace had to be priced, contracted, and staffed from a sheet of paper. The trap in the arithmetic surfaced early too, because a plan drawn at half the length shows a quarter of the floor, and any client who read the second figure as the first underbought the stone.

Proof with Named Properties

The solution below runs in compute mode, with one property naming the license for each inference.

Setup

The hall has actual length L and actual width W in meters, and its area is fixed at 288 square meters. The drawing measures 8 units by 4 units. A single scale k maps each actual side to its drawn side, so the drawn sides are kL and kW. Find k.

Notation

  • A is the actual floor area in square meters, 288.
  • a is the drawn area in square units, 32 once Step 1 fixes it.
  • L is the actual length in meters, a positive real, unknown.
  • W is the actual width in meters, a positive real, unknown.
  • 8 is the drawn length in units, and 4 is the drawn width in units.
  • k is the scale applied to one side, the unknown, a positive real.
  • k² is k multiplied by itself.
  • 1/288 is the multiplicative inverse of 288.
  • √ is the positive square root.
  • ⇒ reads implies.
  • > reads is greater than.
  • x, y, c are any reals, used to state a property in general form.

Step 1 — Definition of the area of a rectangle

Property. Area of a rectangle. Sides x and y give area xy.

Inference. a = (8)(4) = 32.

Step 2 — Definition of a scale factor

Property. Scale factor. A scale k sends each length x of the original to kx in the image.

Inference. 8 = kL and 4 = kW.

Step 3 — Substitution

Property. Substitution. x = y ⇒ x may replace y.

Inference. a = (kL)(kW). From lines 1 and 2.

Step 4 — Commutative and associative laws of multiplication

Property. Commutative and associative laws. (kx)(ky) = (kk)(xy).

Inference. (kL)(kW) = (kk)(LW). From line 3.

Step 5 — Definition of an exponent

Property. Exponent. xx = x².

Inference. a = k²(LW). From line 4.

Step 6 — Substitution, as in Step 3

Property. Substitution, as in Step 3.

Inference. LW = A = 288 and a = 32, so 32 = k²(288). From lines 1 and 5.

Step 7 — Multiplication property of equality

Property. Multiplication property of equality. x = y ⇒ cx = cy.

Inference. (1/288)(32) = (1/288)k²(288). From line 6.

Step 8 — Multiplicative inverse and identity

Property. Multiplicative inverse and multiplicative identity. For x ≠ 0, (1/x)x = 1 and 1x = x.

Inference. k² = 32/288. From line 7.

Step 9 — Cancellation law

Property. Cancellation law. For y ≠ 0, (yx)/y = x.

Inference. 32/288 = 32/(32)(9) = 1/9, so k² = 1/9. From line 8.

Step 10 — Square root property

Property. Square root property. x² = c with c > 0 ⇒ x = √c or x = −√c.

Inference. k = 1/3 or k = −1/3. From line 9.

Step 11 — Order preserved under multiplication by a positive number

Property. Order preserved under multiplication by a positive number. x > 0 and y > 0 ⇒ xy > 0, and x < 0 with y > 0 ⇒ xy < 0.

Inference. L > 0 and kL = 8 > 0 ⇒ k > 0, so k ≠ −1/3 and k = 1/3. From lines 2 and 10.

Verification

k = 1/3 ⇒ L = 8/(1/3) = 24 and W = 4/(1/3) = 12. LW = (24)(12) = 288 = A. Both sides match.

Answer

k = 1/3. Each side of the hall was drawn at one third of its actual length, which is option b.
