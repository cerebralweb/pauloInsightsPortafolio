ONE FIXED TOTAL, TWO RATIOS, NINE FEWER SEATS

In this article ❧ Allocation choices rarely move in isolation. When a fixed pool splits between two groups and the split changes, every unit one side gains is a unit the other side loses, and reading that shift correctly drives staffing, budgeting, and inventory calls. This problem shows how two ratios over one fixed total reduce to a single part value, and how that value exposes the exact loss on either side.

The Problem

Last year, in the school orchestra, there was 1 elementary school student for every 8 high school students. This year the ratio is 2 to 7. The number of spots in the orchestra stays the same. This year there are 18 elementary school students.

How many fewer high school students are there this year?

  • a) 9
  • b) no difference
  • c) 18
  • d) 19
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: a) 9 ✅

Math Solution

Both ratios sum to 9 parts, so the fixed total forces the same part value both years. Dividing 18 by 2 fixes one part at 9, and high school falls from 8 parts to 7 parts, a drop of one part.

When two ratios share one total and sum to the same number of parts, the part value never moves, so the loss equals the change in part count times that value.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Division property of equality: 2q = 18 → q = 18 / 2 = 9. One part this year is 9.
  2. Distributive law: 2q + 7q = (2 + 7)q = 9q. This year total is 9q.
  3. Substitution: 9q = 9 × 9 = 81. This year total is 81.
  4. Substitution, fixed total: 9r = T = 81. Last year total is also 81, since the spots never change.
  5. Division property of equality: 9r = 81 → r = 81 / 9 = 9. One part last year is 9.
  6. Transitivity of equality: r = 9 and q = 9 → r = q. The two part values match.
  7. Substitution: 8r = 8 × 9 = 72 and 7q = 7 × 9 = 63. Last year high school is 72, this year 63.
  8. Distributive law with subtraction: 8r − 7q = (8 − 7) × 9 = 9. The drop is 9.
  9. Verification: 18 + 63 = 81 and 9 + 72 = 81. Both years total 81, so the drop of 9 holds.

The Proof

Mode. Compute.

Setup. Let T be the fixed number of spots. Last year the split was 1 part elementary and 8 parts high school, so 9 equal parts. Let r be one part last year. This year the split is 2 parts elementary and 7 parts high school, so 9 equal parts. Let q be one part this year. This year elementary equals 18, so 2q equals 18. The spots never change, so both years total T. Find 8r minus 7q.

  1. Division property of equality. If a equals b then a over c equals b over c for nonzero c. Equation. 2q = 18 → q = 18 / 2 = 9. One part this year is 9.
  2. Distributive law. c times x plus d times x equals the quantity c plus d times x. Equation. 2q + 7q = (2 + 7)q = 9q. This year total is 9q. Builds on Setup.
  3. Substitution. Equals may replace equals. Equation. 9q = 9 × 9 = 81. This year total is 81. Builds on lines 1 and 2.
  4. Substitution, fixed total. Equals may replace equals. Equation. 9r = T = 81. Last year total is also 81. Builds on line 3 and Setup.
  5. Division property of equality. As in line 1. Equation. 9r = 81 → r = 81 / 9 = 9. One part last year is 9. Builds on line 4.
  6. Transitivity of equality. If a equals b and b equals c then a equals c. Equation. r = 9 and q = 9 → r = q. The two part values match. Builds on lines 1 and 5.
  7. Substitution. Equals may replace equals. Equation. 8r = 8 × 9 = 72 and 7q = 7 × 9 = 63. Last year high school is 72, this year 63. Builds on line 6.
  8. Distributive law with subtraction property of equality. Equation. 8r − 7q = (8 − 7) × 9 = 9. The drop is 9. Builds on lines 6 and 7.

Verification. This year, 18 + 63 = 81. Last year, 9 + 72 = 81. Both totals equal T. High school falls from 72 to 63, a drop of 9.

Answer. There are 9 fewer high school students this year. Answer a.

Historical Background

Ratio and proportion sit among the oldest tools of commerce. Scribes in ancient Mesopotamia used fixed proportions to divide grain, silver, and land, pressing the splits into clay tablets long before symbolic algebra existed. The idea that a whole stays steady while its parts move underlies double entry bookkeeping and modern portfolio allocation alike, which is why a problem about a fixed roster reads the same way an accountant reads a rebalanced budget.
