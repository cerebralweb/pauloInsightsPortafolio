THE FULL BATCH HIDING BEHIND A FIVE PERCENT SHIPMENT

In this article ❧ Businesses often see only a slice of a larger quantity and must reason back to the whole. A shipment, a sample, or a paid deposit can each stand for a known share of something bigger, and that full figure drives planning and forecasts. This problem shows how a single percent relationship lets you recover a total from one known part, and how Python turns that step into a reusable tool.

The Problem

A supplier shipped 30 licenses to Reseller Marta. Those 30 licenses were 5% of all the licenses the supplier had just produced in its latest batch.

How many licenses did the supplier produce in that batch?

  • a) 600
  • b) 300
  • c) 180
  • d) 6,000
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: a) 600 ✅

Math Solution

The shipped amount is a known part of the batch. Since it equals a fixed percent of the whole, you divide the part by that percent written as a decimal to recover the total.

The key move is treating 5% as 0.05, so one division reverses the percent and returns the whole batch.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Convert the percent: 5% becomes 0.05
  2. Set the relationship: 0.05 × T equals 30
  3. Divide each side: T equals 30 ÷ 0.05
  4. Result: T equals 600

Proof with Named Properties

Let T be the total licenses in the batch. The shipped licenses equal 5% of T and equal 30. So the governing relationship is 0.05 × T equals 30. Two paths reach the same total.

Solution 1 — Division path

  1. Definition of percent. A percent is a ratio per hundred, so p% equals p over 100. Here 5% equals 5 over 100 equals 0.05.
  2. Substitution. Equals may replace equals. The relationship becomes 0.05 × T equals 30, using line 1.
  3. Division property of equality. If a equals b and c is nonzero, then a over c equals b over c. So T equals 30 over 0.05, from line 2.
  4. Cancellation law. For nonzero b, b × a over b equals a. Here 30 over 0.05 equals 3000 over 5, which is 5 × 600 over 5, and the 5 cancels, leaving 600. Builds on line 3.
  5. Transitivity of equality. If a equals b and b equals c, then a equals c. Therefore T equals 600, from lines 3 and 4.

Verification. 0.05 × 600 equals 30. The shipped licenses match.

Solution 2 — Unit fraction path

  1. Definition of percent. A percent is a ratio per hundred, so p% equals p over 100. Here 5% equals 5 over 100.
  2. Cancellation law. For nonzero b, b × a over b equals a. Here 5 over 100 equals 5 over 5 × 20, and the 5 cancels, leaving 1 over 20. Builds on line 1.
  3. Substitution. Equals may replace equals. The relationship becomes one over 20 × T equals 30, using line 2.
  4. Multiplication property of equality. If a equals b, then c × a equals c × b. So 20 × the quantity one over 20 × T equals 20 × 30, from line 3.
  5. Multiplicative inverse and identity. A nonzero number times its inverse is one, and one times a equals a. The left side is T, since 20 × one over 20 is one. Builds on line 4.
  6. Product on the right. 20 × 30 equals 600, from line 4.
  7. Transitivity of equality. If a equals b and b equals c, then a equals c. Therefore T equals 600, from lines 5 and 6.

Verification. One over 20 × 600 equals 30. The shipped licenses match. Both paths give a batch of 600 licenses.

Historical Background

Percent thinking grew from Roman revenue counted in hundredths, such as the centesima rerum venalium, a levy of one part in a hundred on goods sold at auction. Merchant houses across medieval Italy later spread per hundred reckoning through their trade ledgers, where recovering a whole from a known share was a daily task. The same reversal, moving from a part back to a total, still sits under pricing, sampling, and inventory planning today.

Python Version

The script recovers a full batch size from any known part and the percent that part represents.

# What this does: works out the full batch size from a part and its percent.
# Where it came from: a supplier shipped some licenses that were only a percent of the whole batch.
# The math idea: turn the percent into a part of one, then divide the known amount by it.
# You type in: the number of shipped licenses and the percent they stand for.
# It prints: the total licenses the supplier produced in the batch.

# This function does the math.
def total_batch(shipped, percent):
    # Turn the percent into a part of one. So 5 becomes 0.05.
    part = percent / 100
    # Divide the shipped amount by that part to get the whole batch.
    return shipped / part

# Tell the reader what we will figure out.
print("Let's find the full batch size from a shipped part.")

# Ask for the shipped licenses.
shipped = float(input("Shipped licenses: "))

# Ask for the percent they represent.
percent = float(input("Percent of the batch shipped: "))

# Do the math.
result = total_batch(shipped, percent)

# Show a whole number when the answer has no leftover.
result = int(result) if result.is_integer() else result

# Print the answer with words that fit the scenario.
print("Licenses produced in the batch:", result)

Run it in a terminal, type the shipped count and the percent, and it returns the total licenses produced in the batch.
