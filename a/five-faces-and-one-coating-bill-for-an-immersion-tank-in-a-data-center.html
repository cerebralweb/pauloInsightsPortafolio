FIVE FACES AND ONE COATING BILL FOR AN IMMERSION TANK IN A DATA CENTER

In this article ❧ Coating a container is priced by area, not by the volume it holds, so the size of the order depends entirely on how much surface must be covered. In data infrastructure an open tank exposes its floor and its walls but never its top, and missing that single detail inflates both the order and the spend. This problem shows how the surface area of an open rectangular prism turns a physical tank into a firm coating budget, and how Python can make that calculation reusable.

The Problem

A cloud operations manager needs to reapply the dielectric coating that covers the entire interior of an immersion cooling tank. The tank is 8 meters long, 6 meters wide, and 2 meters deep. The tank is open at the top, so only the bottom and the four inner walls are coated.

If one square meter of coating costs 600 pesos, how much will the coating cost for the whole tank?

  • a) $91,200
  • b) $6,240
  • c) $9,120
  • d) $62,400
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: d) $62,400 ✅

Math Solution

Add the five interior faces, then multiply by the price per square meter. The bottom gives 48, the two long walls add 32, and the two short walls add 24, for 104 square meters in total. That area times 600 pesos gives the coating bill.

The tank is open, so the top face never enters the bill, and that one exclusion is what separates 104 square meters from 152.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Bottom face. 8 × 6 = 48 square meters
  2. Two long walls. 2 × (8 × 2) = 32 square meters
  3. Two short walls. 2 × (6 × 2) = 24 square meters
  4. Interior area. 48 + 32 + 24 = 104 square meters
  5. Total cost. 104 × 600 = 62,400 pesos

Historical Background

Coating and lining work has long been quoted by the square meter across modern industry, from shipyards to the sealed tanks that now cool dense computing hardware. The practice rests on a shared unit of area that every party can agree on. In 1960 the General Conference on Weights and Measures fixed the square meter within the International System of Units, and that agreement is what lets a supplier in one country and a manager in another settle on a single price for a measured surface.

Python Version

The script computes the interior surface of an open rectangular tank and returns the coating cost, so the same function serves any tank size or price.

def open_tank_coating_cost(length, width, depth, price_per_sqm):
    """Return the coating cost for the interior of an open rectangular tank."""
    bottom = length * width
    long_walls = 2 * (length * depth)
    short_walls = 2 * (width * depth)
    interior_area = bottom + long_walls + short_walls
    return interior_area * price_per_sqm


if __name__ == "__main__":
    cost = open_tank_coating_cost(8, 6, 2, 600)
    print(f"Coating cost: {cost} pesos")

Call it with the tank dimensions and the price per square meter, and it returns 62400 for this tank.
