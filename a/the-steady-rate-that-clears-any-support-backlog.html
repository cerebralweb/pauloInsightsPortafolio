THE STEADY RATE THAT CLEARS ANY SUPPORT BACKLOG

In this article ❧ Support operations run on throughput. A team that knows its steady output per hour can predict when a queue will empty, staff the right number of people, and promise a realistic clearance time to the business. This problem shows how one measured rate turns a growing backlog into a firm estimate, and how Python can hold that logic as a reusable tool.

The Problem

A support team clears tickets at a constant rate. In the first 3 hours the team closes 195 tickets. A backlog of 455 tickets remains.

How many hours will the team need to clear the backlog?

  • a) 7
  • b) 10
  • c) 6
  • d) 5
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: a) 7 ✅

Math Solution

First divide 195 tickets by 3 hours to get the rate of 65 tickets per hour. Then divide the 455 backlog by that rate to get 7 hours.

One measured rate, 65 tickets per hour, converts any queue size into a time estimate.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Rate: 195 ÷ 3 = 65 tickets per hour
  2. Backlog equation: 65 × t = 455
  3. Solve for t: 455 ÷ 65 = 7
  4. Answer: 7 hours

Historical Background

Rate reasoning is one of the oldest tools in commerce. Medieval merchants used the Rule of Three to move from a known price and quantity to an unknown one, and the same pattern ran through ledgers long before algebra reached the counting house.

Industrial work made rates central to planning. Time and motion study measured output per worker per hour, and that figure became the basis for scheduling, staffing, and delivery promises. A modern support queue is the same idea in a new setting.

Python Version

The script measures a rate from a known output and time, then returns the hours needed for any remaining amount.

def hours_to_clear(done, hours_done, remaining):
    rate = done / hours_done
    return remaining / rate


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(hours_to_clear(195, 3, 455))  # 7.0

Run it with your own numbers and it returns the hours to clear the backlog, here 7.0.
