60 OUTPUTS, ONE HOURLY RATE, FIVE HOURS ON THE CLOCK

In this article ❧ Capacity planning turns on one quiet calculation. When a service runs at a known throughput, the time a batch takes falls out of the rate and the target volume, and that number drives schedules, cost estimates, and delivery promises. This problem shows how a single division fixes runtime, and how a short Python script and a Claude skill turn that logic into a tool you can reuse.

The Problem

A GenAI service runs batch inference around the clock. It generates 12 outputs per hour, 24 hours a day, with no pauses or downtime.

How many hours does the service take to generate 60 outputs?

  • a) 5
  • b) 4
  • c) 6
  • d) 4 and a half
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: a) 5 ✅

Math Solution

Read the target as a count of hourly batches. Sixty outputs at 12 per hour means divide 60 by 12, which gives 5. The 24 hour figure never enters the math, since 5 hours fits inside a day.

The rate is the conversion factor. Every 12 outputs buys one hour, so 60 outputs buys five.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Rate: 12 outputs per hour
  2. Target: 60 outputs
  3. Setup: hours = total ÷ rate
  4. Compute: 60 ÷ 12 = 5
  5. Distractor: 24 hours of coverage does not change the result
  6. Answer: 5 hours

Historical Background

Questions of this shape run through commerce long before the term throughput existed. Scribes in ancient Egypt worked division problems of exactly this kind, asking how many fixed portions fill a whole. In 1909 the Danish engineer Agner Krarup Erlang studied how many calls a Copenhagen telephone exchange could carry per hour, and his work turned service rates into a formal field now called queuing theory. The same reasoning that sizes a call center sizes a modern inference queue.

Python Version

The script solves the general case, dividing any target volume by any hourly rate to return the runtime.

# What this does: works out how many hours a service needs to finish a batch.
# Where it came from: a GenAI service makes outputs at a steady rate each hour.
# The math idea: division, total divided by the rate.
# You type in: how many outputs per hour and how many outputs you need.
# It prints: how many hours the service takes to finish.

# This function does the math.
def hours_needed(outputs_per_hour, total_outputs):
    # Divide the total by the hourly rate to get the hours.
    return total_outputs / outputs_per_hour

# Tell the reader what we will figure out.
print("Let's find how many hours the service needs.")

# Ask for the outputs made each hour.
outputs_per_hour = float(input("Outputs per hour: "))

# Ask for the total outputs needed.
total_outputs = float(input("Total outputs needed: "))

# Do the math.
result = hours_needed(outputs_per_hour, total_outputs)

# Show a whole number when it lands even.
result = int(result) if result.is_integer() else result

# Print the answer with words that fit the scenario.
print("Hours to generate all outputs:", result)

Run it in a terminal, type the rate and the target, and it prints the hours required.

# Batch Runtime Estimator

This skill finds how long a steady rate process takes to reach a target volume. It takes two numbers, the amount produced per hour and the total amount needed. It returns the number of hours required, shown as a whole number when the division lands even and as a decimal otherwise.

---

## Background

Difficulty: medium

Two ways to frame the same question.

First framing. "60 songs is how many hours of 12 songs." So 60 = 12x, which gives x = 60 / 12 = 5.

Second framing. "How many hours, at 12 songs per hour, reach 60 songs?" So 12x = 60.

The simplest route uses the 12 times table, since 12 × 5 = 60. Working up the table:

| Count | Hours |
|---|---|
| 12 | 1 |
| 24 | 2 |
| 48 | 4 |
| 48 + 12 = 60 | 5 |

Either framing lands on 5 hours.

## Dependencies

No libraries or external tools are needed. Plain arithmetic covers the whole task.

---

## Inputs

- **Outputs per hour**: how many units the process produces in one hour. A number, may be a decimal.
- **Total outputs needed**: the full count the process must reach. A number, may be a decimal.

---

## Steps

### Step 1 — Greet and state the goal

Tell the user the goal is to find how many hours the process needs to finish the batch.

### Step 2 — Collect the hourly rate

Ask for how many outputs the process produces per hour. Accept a whole number or a decimal.

### Step 3 — Collect the target volume

Ask for the total number of outputs needed. Accept a whole number or a decimal.

### Step 4 — Divide to find the hours

Divide the total volume by the hourly rate. The result is the number of hours required.

### Step 5 — Tidy the result

If the result lands on a whole number, show it as a whole number. Otherwise keep the decimal.

### Step 6 — Report the answer

State the result with context that fits the scenario, for example "Hours to generate all outputs" followed by the number.

## Quality Check Before Delivering

Before returning the answer, verify:

- [ ] The division is total divided by rate, not the reverse
- [ ] A whole number result shows without a trailing decimal
- [ ] The final line names the scenario, not just the bare number
