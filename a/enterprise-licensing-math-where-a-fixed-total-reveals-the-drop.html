ENTERPRISE LICENSING MATH WHERE A FIXED TOTAL REVEALS THE DROP

In this article ❧ License mixes shift when a company reprices or reorganizes its plans. Analysts often know the new mix and one real count, yet not the full picture of what moved underneath. This problem shows how a fixed total turns two separate ratios into one comparable base, and how Python can turn that logic into a reusable tool.

The Problem

A software company sells standard and premium licenses. At first there were 5 standard licenses for every 9 premium licenses. After a plan restructuring, some licenses were dropped. Now there are 11 standard licenses for every 3 premium licenses, and the number of standard licenses is 132. The total license count stayed the same as before the restructuring.

How many more premium licenses were there before?

  • a) 42
  • b) 96
  • c) 84
  • d) 72
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: d) 72 ✅

Math Solution

The known standard count divides by the after share to give 12 groups, so premium after is 3 × 12 = 36 and the total is 168. The fixed total divides by the before group size of 14 to give 12 groups, so premium before is 9 × 12 = 108, and the drop is 108 − 36 = 72.

A fixed total lets one known count set the scale for both mixes at once.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Groups after: 132 ÷ 11 = 12
  2. Premium after: 3 × 12 = 36
  3. Total after: 132 + 36 = 168
  4. Total before: unchanged = 168
  5. Group size before: 5 + 9 = 14
  6. Groups before: 168 ÷ 14 = 12
  7. Premium before: 9 × 12 = 108
  8. Difference: 108 − 36 = 72

Formal Proof

Claim. Under a fixed total license count, the premium tier held 72 more licenses before the restructuring than after it.

Informal sentence Formal sentence
At first there were 5 standard for every 9 premium, then after restructuring 11 standard for every 3 premium with 132 standard, and the total stayed the same, so premium fell by some amount. Let k1, k2 be positive integers. Given standard_before = 5·k1, premium_before = 9·k1, standard_after = 11·k2, premium_after = 3·k2, standard_after = 132, and premium_before + standard_before = premium_after + standard_after, prove that premium_before − premium_after = 72.

Glossary.

  • k1 is the group count of the before mix, a positive integer.
  • k2 is the group count of the after mix, a positive integer.
  • standard_before, premium_before are the two license counts before restructuring.
  • standard_after, premium_after are the two license counts after restructuring.
  • The dot · denotes multiplication.
  • = denotes equality of quantities.

Givens.

  • standard_before = 5·k1 and premium_before = 9·k1.
  • standard_after = 11·k2 and premium_after = 3·k2.
  • standard_after = 132.
  • premium_before + standard_before = premium_after + standard_after.

Goal. premium_before − premium_after = 72.

Numbered Proof

  1. [Substitution into standard_after = 11·k2 using standard_after = 132] 132 = 11·k2.
  2. [Division of both sides of line 1 by 11] k2 = 12.
  3. [Substitution of k2 = 12 into premium_after = 3·k2] premium_after = 3·12 = 36.
  4. [Substitution of standard_after = 132 and premium_after = 36 into the after total] premium_after + standard_after = 36 + 132 = 168.
  5. [Substitution of line 4 into the equal totals given] premium_before + standard_before = 168.
  6. [Substitution of standard_before = 5·k1 and premium_before = 9·k1 into line 5] 9·k1 + 5·k1 = 168.
  7. [Addition of like terms in line 6] 14·k1 = 168.
  8. [Division of both sides of line 7 by 14] k1 = 12.
  9. [Substitution of k1 = 12 into premium_before = 9·k1] premium_before = 9·12 = 108.
  10. [Subtraction of premium_after from premium_before using lines 9 and 3] premium_before − premium_after = 108 − 36 = 72.

Verification. Line 10 gives premium_before − premium_after = 72, which is the goal. The before total 108 + 60 = 168 equals the after total 36 + 132 = 168, so the fixed total holds. The claim is established.

Python Version

The script solves the type, not the instance, so any valid before mix, after mix, and known count returns the premium drop.

# What this does: finds how many more premium licenses there were before a restructuring.
# Where it came from: a company changed its license mix, one count is known, and the total license count stayed the same.
# The math idea: ratios, scaled to real numbers, then a subtraction.
# You type in: the two before numbers, the two after numbers, and the known standard count after.
# It prints: how many more premium licenses there were before.

# This function does the math.
def premium_drop(std_before, prem_before, std_after, prem_after, std_after_count):
    # Find how many groups fit the after mix, using the known standard count.
    groups_after = std_after_count / std_after
    # Build the premium count after from those groups.
    premium_after = prem_after * groups_after
    # Build the total after. The total stays the same before and after.
    total = std_after_count + premium_after
    # Find how many groups fit the before mix inside that same total.
    groups_before = total / (std_before + prem_before)
    # Build the premium count before from those groups.
    premium_before = prem_before * groups_before
    # The answer is the drop in premium licenses.
    return premium_before - premium_after

# Tell the reader what we will figure out.
print("Let's find how many more premium licenses there were before.")

# Ask for the before mix.
std_before = float(input("Standard per group, before: "))
prem_before = float(input("Premium per group, before: "))

# Ask for the after mix.
std_after = float(input("Standard per group, after: "))
prem_after = float(input("Premium per group, after: "))

# Ask for the known standard count after.
std_after_count = float(input("Standard licenses after: "))

# Do the math.
result = premium_drop(std_before, prem_before, std_after, prem_after, std_after_count)

# Show a whole number when the result has no fraction.
result = int(result) if result.is_integer() else result

# Print the answer with words that fit the scenario.
print("Extra premium licenses before the restructuring:", result)

Run it, type 5, 9, 11, 3, and 132, and it returns 72.

# License Mix Premium Drop

This skill finds how many more premium licenses a company held before a restructuring than after. It takes two license mixes stated as ratios, one before and one after, plus the known standard license count after the change. It assumes the total license count stayed the same across the change. It returns the drop in premium licenses.

---

## Dependencies

No libraries are needed. Plain arithmetic only.

---

## Inputs

- **Standard per group, before**. The standard share of the before mix.
- **Premium per group, before**. The premium share of the before mix.
- **Standard per group, after**. The standard share of the after mix.
- **Premium per group, after**. The premium share of the after mix.
- **Standard licenses after**. The real standard count once the change is done.

---

## Steps

### Step 1 — Find the after group count

Divide the known standard count after by the standard share of the after mix. This tells you how many equal groups the after mix breaks into.

### Step 2 — Build the premium count after

Multiply the after group count by the premium share of the after mix. This gives the real premium count after the change.

### Step 3 — Build the shared total

Add the known standard count after and the premium count after. This total holds for both the before state and the after state, since the total stayed the same.

### Step 4 — Find the before group count

Add the standard and premium shares of the before mix to get the before group size. Divide the shared total by that group size. This tells you how many groups the before mix breaks into.

### Step 5 — Build the premium count before

Multiply the before group count by the premium share of the before mix. This gives the real premium count before the change.

### Step 6 — Return the drop

Subtract the premium count after from the premium count before. Report this as the extra premium licenses that existed before the restructuring. Show a whole number when the result has no fraction.
