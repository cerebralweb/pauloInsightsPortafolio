WHEN THE ARCHITECTURE POSTER SHRINKS TO FIT A CLIENT DECK

In this article ❧ Technical drawings travel. A diagram printed for a wall becomes a slide, a handout, and a page in a proposal, and every move changes its size while the relationships inside it must stay intact. The size relationship between an original drawing and any copy of it is the scale factor, and a team that states it once can resize with confidence and hand the work to a short Python function.

The Problem

A cloud team prints a poster of its reference architecture. On the poster, the object storage node is drawn as a circle with a diameter of 24 cm. For the client deck, a designer produces a reduced copy of the same drawing, where the object storage node appears as a circle with a radius of 6 cm.

What is the scale factor of the reduced copy relative to the poster?

  • a) 1/4
  • b) 1/2
  • c) 2
  • d) 1/8
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: b) 1/2 ✅

Math Solution

The two figures report different measures, so the copy needs a conversion from radius to diameter before any comparison is valid. With both figures stated as diameters, the scale factor is the quotient of the copy diameter and the original diameter, which reduces to 1/2.

Every circle is similar to every other circle, so one factor governs the whole drawing once a single pair of matching lengths is compared.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Copy diameter: (2)(6) = 12 cm
  2. Matching measures: original 24 cm, copy 12 cm, both diameters
  3. Scale factor: 12 / 24
  4. Reduced form: 12 / 24 = 1/2
  5. Check: (1/2)(24) = 12, and (2)(6) = 12

The Proof

The breakdown above gives the arithmetic. The sequence below gives the licence for each move, with one named property per step.

Setup

The original drawing shows a circle whose diameter is 24 cm. The copy shows the corresponding circle with a radius of 6 cm. Let the original diameter be D, the copy radius r, the copy diameter d, and the scale factor k. The scale factor compares one length of the copy with the corresponding length of the original, so k = d/D.

Notation

  • D is the diameter of the circle in the original drawing, 24 cm.
  • r is the radius of the corresponding circle in the copy, 6 cm.
  • d is the diameter of that circle in the copy, fixed at 12 cm in Step 1.
  • k is the scale factor of the copy relative to the original, the unknown, a positive rational.
  • x, y, c are any real numbers, used to state a property in general form.
  • ⇒ reads implies.
  • ≠ reads is not equal to.

Solution

  1. Definition of the diameter of a circle. Property. For a circle of radius x, the diameter is 2x. Inference. d = (2)6 = 12.

  2. Similarity of circles. Property. All circles are similar, so one is an enlargement of the other. Inference. The copy circle and the original circle are similar, so a single k governs every pair of corresponding lengths. From line 1.

  3. Definition of the scale factor of similar figures. Property. For similar figures, corresponding lengths x and y satisfy k = x/y, the same k for every such pair. Inference. k = d/D = 12/24. From lines 1 and 2.

  4. Cancellation law. Property. For y ≠ 0, (yx)/y = x. Inference. k = 12/24 = (12)(1)/(12)(2) = 1/2. From line 3.

Verification

Substitute k = 1/2 into d = kD. (1/2)(24) = 12, and 2r = (2)6 = 12. Both sides match.

Answer

k = 1/2, option b. The copy is drawn at half the size of the original.

Historical Background

Drawing to scale is one of the oldest working uses of proportion. Roman builders and their heirs in Renaissance Italy set out plans on a fixed module, so a length on the sheet stood for a known length on the site and any craftsman could read the building from the drawing. Vitruvius wrote the practice down for architects, and Alberti later formalised it for the workshops of Florence, which is where the modern habit of stating a drawing's reduction on the drawing itself begins.

Python Version

The script converts the measures of a copy and an original to a common basis, then returns the single factor that relates them, which makes it reusable for any resized drawing.

def scale_factor(original_diameter, copy_radius):
    """Return the scale factor of a copy relative to an original circle."""
    copy_diameter = 2 * copy_radius
    return copy_diameter / original_diameter


if __name__ == "__main__":
    k = scale_factor(24, 6)
    print(k)
    print(f"The copy is drawn at {k:.2f} of the original size.")

Run it with python scale_factor.py and it returns 0.5, the decimal form of 1/2.
