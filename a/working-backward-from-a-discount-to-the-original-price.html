WORKING BACKWARD FROM A DISCOUNT TO THE ORIGINAL PRICE

In this article ❧ Pricing teams often see the effect of a markdown before they confirm the number it came from. Recovering the base price lets finance check that a promotion was set correctly and reconcile the money saved against the plan. This problem shows how one division recovers a full price from a discount, and how the same logic becomes a reusable program and a skill.

The Problem

A SaaS team ran a promotion on an annual subscription. The plan was offered at 30% off.

If the discount lowered the price by 24,000, what was the original subscription price?

  • a) 72,000
  • b) 300,000
  • c) 80,000
  • d) 88,000
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: c) 80,000 ✅

Math Solution

The discount is a known part of an unknown base. Writing the rate as 0.30 gives 0.30 times the price equals 24,000, so dividing 24,000 by 0.30 recovers the full price.

The discount amount and the rate are known, so the base is found by division, not multiplication.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Substitution: replace the discount amount with its value, so 0.30 times the price equals 24,000.
  2. Multiplication property of equality: multiply each side by 1 over 0.30.
  3. Multiplicative inverse and identity: the left side collapses to the price alone.
  4. Cancellation law: the right side, 24,000 over 0.30, reduces to 80,000.
  5. Transitivity of equality: therefore the price equals 80,000.

Property Named Solution

Mode. Compute.

Setup. Let p be the original price, a positive rational. The discount rate is 30%, written 0.30. The discount amount is 0.30 times p. The problem fixes the discount amount at 24,000. So 0.30 times p equals 24,000.

Solution.

  1. Substitution. Equals may replace equals. The governing equation is 0.30 times p equals 24,000. The discount amount 0.30 times p is replaced by its stated value 24,000.
  2. Multiplication property of equality. If a equals b then c times a equals c times b. One over 0.30 times the quantity 0.30 times p equals one over 0.30 times 24,000. Builds on step 1. The same factor multiplies each side.
  3. Multiplicative inverse and identity. A nonzero number times its inverse is one, and one times a equals a. The left side equals p. Builds on step 2. One over 0.30 times 0.30 is one, and one times p is p.
  4. Cancellation law. For nonzero b, b times a over b equals a. The right side equals 80,000. 24,000 over 0.30 is 0.30 times 80,000 over 0.30, and the 0.30 cancels, leaving 80,000.
  5. Transitivity of equality. If a equals b and b equals c then a equals c. Therefore p equals 80,000. Builds on steps 3 and 4.

Verification. 0.30 times 80,000 is 24,000. The computed discount matches the stated discount amount.

Answer. The original price was 80,000, option c.

The Substitution Property, Explained

Substitution says that when two things are equal, one can stand in for the other anywhere without changing the truth of a statement. If a equals b, then any statement true of a stays true when b is written in its place. The two names point to the same value, so the swap changes nothing real.

In the setup, the discount was written two ways. As an expression it was 0.30 times the price. As a stated value it was 24,000. The problem said these are equal, so substitution let one replace the other and form a single equation. The property does no arithmetic. It only lets equal quantities trade places so the equation can be assembled.

Historical Background

Reverse percentage work rests on treating a percent as a plain part of a whole, an idea that spread once decimals became routine. In 1585 the Flemish mathematician Simon Stevin published a short work arguing that fractions could be handled as decimals, which made rate based figures far easier to compute.

His approach moved quickly into banking and trade, where discounts, interest, and markups were daily practice. The habit of turning a percent into a decimal part, then dividing or multiplying, traces back to that shift in how numbers were written.

Python Version

This script solves the same type of problem for any discount amount and any percent, so it can be reopened and reused later.

# What this does: works out the original price from a discount and its percent.
# Where it came from: a SaaS plan was offered at a percent off, and only the money saved is known.
# The math idea: divide the money saved by the percent, written as a part of one.
# You type in: the discount amount and the discount percent.
# It prints: the original price before the discount.

# This function does the math.
def original_price(discount_amount, discount_percent):
    # Turn the percent into a part of one. So 30 becomes 0.30.
    discount_part = discount_percent / 100
    # Divide the money saved by that part to get back the full price.
    return discount_amount / discount_part

# Tell the reader what we will figure out.
print("Let's find the original price before a discount.")

# Ask for the discount amount.
discount_amount = float(input("Discount amount: "))

# Ask for the discount percent.
discount_percent = float(input("Discount percent: "))

# Do the math.
result = original_price(discount_amount, discount_percent)

# Show a whole number when the result has no cents.
result = int(result) if result.is_integer() else round(result, 2)

# Print the answer with words that fit the scenario.
print("Original price before the discount:", result)

Run it, type 24000 for the amount and 30 for the percent, and it prints 80000.

# Reverse Percentage Price Finder

This skill finds the original price of an item when only the money saved and the discount percent are known. It asks for the discount amount and the discount percent, then works backward to the full price before the discount. The output is a single price stated in plain business terms.

---

## Dependencies

None. Uses basic arithmetic only.

---

## Inputs

- **Discount amount**: the money removed by the discount, a number that may include decimals.
- **Discount percent**: the percent off, entered as a plain number such as 30 for thirty percent.

---

## Steps

### Step 1 — Collect the two inputs

Ask for the discount amount in money. Then ask for the discount percent. Accept decimals for both.

### Step 2 — Convert the percent to a part of one

Divide the discount percent by 100. This turns a percent into a fraction of the whole price. For example, 30 becomes 0.30.

### Step 3 — Recover the original price

Divide the discount amount by the part from Step 2. The result is the full price before the discount. Division reverses the multiplication that produced the discount.

### Step 4 — Present the result

If the price comes out to a whole number, show it without decimals. Otherwise round to two decimals. State it with scenario text, for example "Original price before the discount: 80000".

---

## Example Trigger Prompts

- "I know the amount saved and the percent. Find the price before the sale."
- "Work backward from a discount to the original price."
- "A markdown removed a certain amount at a set percent. What did it cost first?"
