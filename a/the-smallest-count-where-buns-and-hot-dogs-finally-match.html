THE SMALLEST COUNT WHERE BUNS AND HOT DOGS FINALLY MATCH

In this article ❧ Inventory planning often hinges on making two supply streams line up. When items arrive in fixed pack sizes, a manager who wants a clean pairing with nothing left over must find the point where both counts meet. This problem shows how the least common multiple turns two pack sizes into the smallest order that pairs every unit, and how Python can make that logic reusable.

The Problem

A package of hot dog buns contains 10 buns, and a package of hot dogs contains 8.

How many of each should you buy to make hot dogs without any leftovers?

  • a) 4 and 5
  • b) 5 and 4
  • c) 30 and 32
  • d) 4 and 4
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: a) 4 and 5 ✅

Math Solution

The two pack sizes must reach a common total, so the smallest shared count is the least common multiple of the two sizes. Factor each size into primes, take the highest power of each prime, and multiply to get 40. Then divide 40 by each pack size to find the number of packs.

The number 40 is the first total that both pack sizes divide evenly, which is exactly what the least common multiple gives.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Factor the sizes: 10 is 2 times 5, and 8 is 2 cubed.
  2. Least common multiple: 2 cubed times 5 equals 40.
  3. Bun packs: 40 divided by 10 equals 4.
  4. Hot dog packs: 40 divided by 8 equals 5.
  5. Result: 4 bun packs and 5 hot dog packs, giving 40 of each with no leftovers.

Proof With Named Properties

Mode. Compute.

Setup. Let m be the number of bun packages and n the number of hot dog packages, both positive integers. Each bun package holds 10 and each hot dog package holds 8. No leftovers means buns equal hot dogs, so 10m equals 8n. We want the smallest positive integer solution.

Step 1 — Fundamental theorem of arithmetic

Property. Every integer greater than one factors uniquely into primes.

Inference. 10 equals 2 times 5, and 8 equals 2 cubed.

Step 2 — Definition of least common multiple

Property. The least common multiple of two integers is the product of each prime raised to the highest power appearing in either factorization.

Inference. The least common multiple of 10 and 8 is 2 cubed times 5, which is 40. Builds on Step 1. So 40 is the smallest count that both 10 and 8 divide, fixing 10m equals 8n equals 40.

Step 3 — Cancellation law

Property. For nonzero b, b times a over b equals a.

Inference. Bun packages m equals 40 over 10 equals 4. 40 over 10 is 10 times 4 over 10, and the 10 cancels, leaving 4. Builds on Step 2.

Step 4 — Cancellation law

Property. As in Step 3.

Inference. Hot dog packages n equals 40 over 8 equals 5. 40 over 8 is 8 times 5 over 8, and the 8 cancels, leaving 5. Builds on Step 2.

Verification. 10 times 4 equals 40, and 8 times 5 equals 40. Buns equal hot dogs, so no leftovers.

Answer. Buy 4 packages of buns and 5 packages of hot dogs, giving 40 of each. Option a.

Historical Background

The search for a shared multiple is one of the oldest questions in arithmetic. Greek mathematicians in Euclid's Elements studied how two quantities relate through their common measures and multiples, work that later fed into calendar design and the meshing of gear teeth. Traders and planners have long used the same idea to align batches of goods that arrive in different lot sizes, so that stock pairs cleanly with no surplus.

Python Version

The script below computes the smallest matched order for any two pack sizes, so it works far beyond buns and hot dogs.

from math import gcd

def packages_without_leftovers(buns_per_pack, dogs_per_pack):
    total = buns_per_pack * dogs_per_pack // gcd(buns_per_pack, dogs_per_pack)
    bun_packs = total // buns_per_pack
    dog_packs = total // dogs_per_pack
    return bun_packs, dog_packs

print(packages_without_leftovers(10, 8))

Run it with the two pack sizes and it returns the number of packs for each, here four and five.
