FOUR CAMPAIGNS, ONE DAILY RATE, AND THE SLOWEST SPENDER REVEALED

In this article ❧ Marketing teams rarely compare campaigns on equal footing. Each one runs for a different stretch and spends a different amount, so raw totals mislead. Reducing every campaign to a single daily rate makes unequal runs directly comparable, and Python turns that comparison into a tool you can reuse.

The Problem

Four ad campaigns each burned budget over a set number of days. The red campaign spent 10,000 pesos over 4 days. The blue campaign spent 12,000 pesos over 6 days. The green campaign spent 14,000 pesos over 6 days. The yellow campaign spent 14,000 pesos over 6 days.

Which campaign burns budget slowest?

  • a) blue
  • b) yellow
  • c) green
  • d) red
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: a) blue ✅

Math Solution

Convert each campaign to pesos per day by dividing spend by days. Red is 2,500, blue is 2,000, green and yellow are about 2,333, so blue holds the smallest rate.

The trick is the shared unit. Once every campaign is stated in pesos per day, the ranking is immediate.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Red rate: 10,000 ÷ 4 = 2,500 pesos per day
  2. Blue rate: 12,000 ÷ 6 = 2,000 pesos per day
  3. Green rate: 14,000 ÷ 6 ≈ 2,333 pesos per day
  4. Yellow rate: 14,000 ÷ 6 ≈ 2,333 pesos per day
  5. Compare: 2,000 < 2,333 < 2,500, so blue is slowest

Rigorous Proof

This is the same result built as named property steps. Mode: compute.

Setup. Let the burn rates in pesos per day be r_red, r_blue, r_green, and r_yellow. A rate is spend divided by days. The pairs are red 10,000 over 4, blue 12,000 over 6, green 14,000 over 6, and yellow 14,000 over 6. The task is to find the smallest rate.

  1. Cancellation law. For nonzero b, b times a over b equals a. So r_red is 2,500. 10,000 over 4 is 4 times 2,500 over 4, and the 4 cancels.
  2. Cancellation law, as in step 1. r_blue is 2,000. 12,000 over 6 is 6 times 2,000 over 6, and the 6 cancels.
  3. Cancellation law, as in step 1. r_green is 7,000 over 3, about 2,333.33. 14,000 over 6 is 2 times 7,000 over 2 times 3, and the 2 cancels.
  4. Substitution. Equals may replace equals. r_yellow equals 7,000 over 3, the same value as r_green, since yellow shares the pair 14,000 over 6. Builds on step 3.
  5. Transitivity of order. If a is less than b and b is less than c, then a is less than c. Here 2,000 is less than 7,000 over 3, and 7,000 over 3 is less than 2,500. Builds on steps 1 through 4. So r_blue is below r_green, which is below r_red.
  6. Trichotomy. For any two reals, exactly one of less than, equal, or greater than holds. r_blue is the unique minimum. Green and yellow tie above it, and red sits higher. Builds on step 5.

Verification. Substitute r_blue into the constraint. 2,000 times 6 equals 12,000, which matches the blue spend. And 2,000 is below both 2,500 and 7,000 over 3, so blue holds the smallest rate.

Answer. Blue burns budget slowest at 2,000 pesos per day. Option a.

Historical Background

The idea of reducing quantities to a rate per single unit runs through modern science. In 1822 Joseph Fourier, studying how heat moves, argued that every physical quantity carries units and that those units must balance on both sides of any comparison. That principle, later called dimensional analysis, is what lets a manager set spend per day beside spend per day and trust the ranking.

Python Version

The script asks for each campaign and reports the slowest daily spender, so it works for any number of campaigns, not just four.

# What this does. finds which campaign burns budget slowest.
# Where it came from. four ad campaigns each spent some pesos over some days.
# The math idea. turn each spend and days into pesos per day, then keep the smallest.
# You type in. how many campaigns, then each name, its spend, and its days.
# It prints. the campaign with the smallest pesos per day.

# This function does the math.
def daily_burn(spend, days):
    # Divide the spend by the days to get pesos per day.
    return spend / days

# Tell the reader what we will figure out.
print("Let's find which campaign burns budget slowest.")

# Ask how many campaigns there are.
count = int(input("How many campaigns "))

# Start with no winner yet.
slowest_name = ""
slowest_rate = None

# Look at each campaign one at a time.
for i in range(count):
    # Ask for this campaign name.
    name = input("Campaign name ")

    # Ask for this campaign spend in pesos.
    spend = float(input("Spend in pesos "))

    # Ask for how many days it ran.
    days = float(input("Days it ran "))

    # Work out the pesos per day.
    rate = daily_burn(spend, days)

    # Keep this one if it is the smallest so far.
    if slowest_rate is None or rate < slowest_rate:
        slowest_rate = rate
        slowest_name = name

# Round the rate to two decimals for money.
slowest_rate = round(slowest_rate, 2)

# Print the answer with words that fit the scenario.
print("Slowest campaign", slowest_name, "at", slowest_rate, "pesos per day")

Run it in a terminal, enter each campaign, and it prints the slowest campaign with its pesos per day.

# Campaign Burn Comparator

This skill compares any number of campaigns by their daily burn. For each campaign it takes a spend and the days it ran, converts them to pesos per day, and reports the campaign with the smallest rate. Input is a count and one name, spend, and days per campaign. Output is a single line naming the slowest campaign and its rate.

---

## Dependencies

None. Plain arithmetic only.

---

## Inputs

- **Campaign count.** A whole number of campaigns to compare.
- **Campaign name.** A label for each campaign.
- **Spend.** The pesos each campaign spent. May be a decimal.
- **Days.** The days each campaign ran. May be a decimal.

---

## Steps

### Step 1. Ask how many campaigns

Ask the user for the number of campaigns to compare. Expect a whole number.

### Step 2. Collect each campaign

For each campaign, ask for its name, its spend in pesos, and the days it ran. Spend and days may be decimals.

### Step 3. Compute the daily burn

For each campaign, divide the spend by the days to get pesos per day.

### Step 4. Track the smallest rate

Keep the campaign with the lowest pesos per day. If a campaign ties the current lowest, keep the first one seen.

### Step 5. Report the result

Round the winning rate to two decimals. Print one line naming the slowest campaign and its rate in pesos per day.
