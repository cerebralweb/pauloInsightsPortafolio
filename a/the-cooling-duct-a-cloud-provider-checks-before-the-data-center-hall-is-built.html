THE COOLING DUCT A CLOUD PROVIDER CHECKS BEFORE THE DATA CENTER HALL IS BUILT

In this article ❧ Infrastructure decisions are reviewed long before anything is poured or installed. Facilities teams, plant engineers, and equipment vendors work from models and drawings, where every dimension carries a fixed relationship to the object that will exist later, and where the reported unit is rarely the unit the design was measured in. This problem shows how one shrinking factor and one unit equivalence settle a dimension a review board can act on, and how a short Python function turns that logic into a reusable tool.

The Problem

A facilities team at a cloud provider is building a physical scale model of a new data center hall for a design review. In the actual hall, the circular cooling duct opening has a diameter of 5 feet. The model is built at a scale factor of 1/30.

The diameter of that opening on the model will be [1 foot = 12 inches]

  • a) 60 inches
  • b) 30 inches
  • c) 4 inches
  • d) 2 inches
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: d) 2 inches ✅

Math Solution

The model and the hall are similar figures, so the duct diameter shrinks by the scale factor, giving 5 ✕ 1/30 = 1/6 of a foot, and that result is then converted into inches by 12 ✕ 1/6 = 2.

Every 30 feet in the hall becomes 1 foot on the model, which is 12 inches, so each actual foot is worth two fifths of an inch.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Actual duct diameter: 5 feet across the opening in the hall
  2. Scale factor: 1/30, applied to every length on the model
  3. Model diameter in feet: 5 ✕ 1/30 = 1/6 of a foot
  4. Unit equivalence: 1 foot = 12 inches
  5. Model diameter in inches: 12 ✕ 1/6 = 2 inches
  6. Check: 2 inches is 1/6 of a foot, and (1/6) ÷ 5 = 1/30, the scale factor
  7. Answer: the duct opening measures 2 inches across on the model

Property by Property Solution

Mode. Compute.

Setup. Let D be the actual duct diameter, D = 5 ft. Let k be the scale factor, k = 1/30. Let m be the model diameter in feet and M the same diameter in inches. The model opening and the actual opening are similar circles. Report M.

Notation

  • D is the actual duct diameter, 5 feet.
  • k is the scale factor from hall to model, one thirtieth.
  • m is the model diameter in feet, fixed in Step 3.
  • M is the model diameter in inches, the unknown, a positive rational.
  • 12 is the number of inches in one foot.
  • The symbol = states that two quantities are the same number.

Solution

  1. Step 1 — Corresponding lengths in similar figures.

    Property. If two figures are similar with scale factor k, then any length L in the first and its corresponding length L′ in the second satisfy L′ = kL.

    Inference. m = kD.

  2. Step 2 — Substitution.

    Property. If a = b, then a may replace b in any true statement.

    Inference. m = (1/30)(5). Builds on line 1.

  3. Step 3 — Cancellation law.

    Property. For b ≠ 0, (ba)/b = a.

    Inference. m = 1/6 ft. Builds on line 2, since 5/30 = 5/(5 · 6) = 1/6.

  4. Step 4 — Definition of the inch, with substitution.

    Property. 1 ft = 12 in, so a length of x ft equals 12x in.

    Inference. M = 12(1/6). Builds on line 3.

  5. Step 5 — Associativity of multiplication.

    Property. (ab)c = a(bc).

    Inference. M = (2 · 6)(1/6) = 2(6 · 1/6). Builds on line 4.

  6. Step 6 — Multiplicative inverse and multiplicative identity.

    Property. For a ≠ 0, a · (1/a) = 1, and 1 · a = a.

    Inference. M = 2. Builds on line 5, since 6 · 1/6 = 1 and 2 · 1 = 2.

  7. Step 7 — Transitivity of equality.

    Property. If a = b and b = c, then a = c.

    Inference. M = 2 in. Builds on lines 4 through 6.

Verification

M = 2 in gives m = 2/12 = 1/6 ft. Then m/D = (1/6)/5 = 1/30 = k. The ratio equals the scale factor.

Answer

The duct opening measures 2 inches across on the model, option d.

Historical Background

Reducing a real structure to a drawing a reviewer can hold is an old administrative habit. Roman surveyors and architects worked from measured plans, and around the year 203 the city of Rome was carved onto a marble wall as the Forma Urbis Romae, a floor plan of streets, warehouses, and public buildings reduced to a fraction of their true size.

The same practice ran through the building yards of medieval Europe, where master masons priced stone and timber from templates and plans rather than from the finished wall. Industrial procurement kept the method, because a fraction that holds across every dimension lets one measurement settle a material order, a clearance, or a fabrication tolerance.

Python Version

The script applies any scale factor to any length in feet and returns the model dimension in inches, so a facilities team can run it once per component instead of redoing the conversion by hand.

from fractions import Fraction


def model_length_in_inches(actual_feet, scale_factor):
    """Scale a length in feet, then report it in inches.

    actual_feet   length of the real component, in feet
    scale_factor  ratio of model to actual, e.g. Fraction(1, 30)
    """
    model_feet = Fraction(actual_feet) * Fraction(scale_factor)
    return model_feet * 12


if __name__ == "__main__":
    duct = model_length_in_inches(5, Fraction(1, 30))
    print(duct)          # 2
    print(float(duct))   # 2.0

Run it with python model_length.py, and it returns 2, the duct diameter in inches on a 1/30 model. Exact fractions keep the value clean, so a 1/30 or a 1/48 model reports without rounding drift.
