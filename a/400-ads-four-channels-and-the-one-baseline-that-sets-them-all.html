400 ADS, FOUR CHANNELS, AND THE ONE BASELINE THAT SETS THEM ALL

In this article ❧ Marketing teams rarely set channel volumes one by one. They tie every channel to a single baseline, then let fixed multiples do the rest, which keeps a campaign balanced as the budget shifts. This problem shows how one linear equation recovers that hidden baseline, and how Python turns the same logic into a tool a planner can reuse.

The Problem

A marketing manager needs 10 times as many display ads as video ads. He needs the same number of social media ads as video ads. The number of search ads is half the number of video ads. The manager sets aside 400 ads for the campaign.

How many video ads does he need?

  • a) 320
  • b) 32
  • c) 16
  • d) 3.2
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: b) 32 ✅

Math Solution

Write each channel as a multiple of the video count, then add the coefficients into one factor. Divide the total of 400 by that factor to recover the baseline.

Every channel collapses into a single coefficient of 12.5, so the whole campaign rests on one division.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Express each channel: display 10v, video v, social v, search 0.5v
  2. Sum the coefficients: 10 + 1 + 1 + 0.5 = 12.5
  3. Set the equation: 12.5v = 400
  4. Divide: 400 / 12.5 = 32
  5. Video ads: 32

Named Property Proof

The same result holds under a chain where every step names the property that licenses it. Let v be the number of video ads, a positive rational. Display equals 10v, social equals v, search equals one half v, and the total is 400.

Step 1 — Distributive law

Property. Distributive law. c times a plus c times b equals c times the quantity a plus b.

Inference. 10v plus v plus v plus one half v equals the quantity 10 plus 1 plus 1 plus one half, times v. Every term shares the factor v.

Step 2 — Associative law of addition

Property. Associative law of addition. Regrouping a sum does not change it.

Inference. The coefficient equals 25 over 2. 10 plus 1 plus 1 is 12, and 12 plus one half is 25 over 2. Builds on line 1.

Step 3 — Substitution

Property. Substitution. Equals may replace equals.

Inference. 25 over 2 times v equals 400. Builds on lines 1 and 2, replacing the coefficient sum with 25 over 2.

Step 4 — Multiplication property of equality

Property. Multiplication property of equality. If a equals b then c times a equals c times b.

Inference. 2 over 25 times the quantity 25 over 2 times v equals 2 over 25 times 400. Builds on line 3. The same factor multiplies each side.

Step 5 — Multiplicative inverse and identity

Property. Multiplicative inverse property and multiplicative identity property. A nonzero number times its inverse is one, and one times a equals a.

Inference. The left side equals v. Builds on line 4. 2 over 25 times 25 over 2 is one, and one times v is v.

Step 6 — Cancellation law

Property. Cancellation law. For nonzero b, b times a over b equals a.

Inference. The right side equals 32. 2 times 400 is 800, and 800 over 25 is 25 times 32 over 25, so the 25 cancels, leaving 32. Builds on line 4.

Step 7 — Transitivity of equality

Property. Transitivity of equality. If a equals b and b equals c then a equals c.

Inference. Therefore v equals 32. Builds on lines 5 and 6.

Substituting v equals 32 gives display 320, video 32, social 32, and search 16, which sum to 400. The value satisfies the constraint.

Historical Background

Merchants solved this kind of task long before algebra through the rule of three, a proportion method that spread across medieval trade routes and reckoning schools. Italian abacus masters used it to convert quantities, currencies, and shares from a single known relation. The same instinct, tie many values to one baseline, still drives how a planner sizes a modern campaign.

Python Version

The script takes the total and the channel multiples, then returns the baseline count along with every channel volume.

from fractions import Fraction

def solve_campaign(total, multiples):
    factor = sum(Fraction(m) for m in multiples.values())
    base = Fraction(total) / factor
    channels = {name: base * Fraction(m) for name, m in multiples.items()}
    return channels, base

multiples = {"display": 10, "video": 1, "social": 1, "search": Fraction(1, 2)}
channels, video = solve_campaign(400, multiples)
print(video)        # 32
print(channels)     # display 320, video 32, social 32, search 16

Run it with any total and multiple set, and it returns the video baseline of 32 along with the full channel split.
