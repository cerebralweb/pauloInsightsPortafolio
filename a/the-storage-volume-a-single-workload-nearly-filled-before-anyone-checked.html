THE STORAGE VOLUME A SINGLE WORKLOAD NEARLY FILLED BEFORE ANYONE CHECKED

In this article ❧ Provisioned capacity is paid for in advance, so the only figure that matters in a review is how much of it a workload is actually holding. Teams that track the share consumed see a volume approaching its ceiling weeks before a write fails, while teams that track raw volume consumed see nothing until the alert fires. This problem shows how one part over one whole, restated per hundred, turns a storage reading into a capacity decision, and how every step of that reading rests on a named property rather than on habit.

The Problem

Juan's workload wrote 424.15 GB to the storage volume provisioned for Diego's project, which had a capacity of 499.05 GB.

What percentage of the volume did Juan's workload consume?

  • a) 85 %
  • b) 80 %
  • c) 71 %
  • d) 77 %
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: a) 85 % ✅

Math Solution

The consumed volume divided by the provisioned capacity gives the share held, and that share multiplied by 100 states it per hundred. Clearing the decimals first keeps the arithmetic exact, so 424.15 over 499.05 becomes 8483 over 9981.

The volume is at 84.99 of every 100 GB provisioned, which leaves 74.9 GB of headroom, one bad write away from a capacity event.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Part and whole: consumed 424.15 GB, provisioned 499.05 GB
  2. Share held: 424.15 / 499.05
  3. Decimals cleared: (100)(424.15) / (100)(499.05) = 42415 / 49905
  4. Common factor removed: (5)(8483) / (5)(9981) = 8483 / 9981
  5. Stated per hundred: (100)(8483 / 9981) = 848300 / 9981
  6. Whole part and remainder: 848300 = (9981)(84) + 9896, so 84 + 9896 / 9981
  7. Decimal value: 84.9915
  8. Rounded reading: 85
  9. Check: (0.849915)(499.05) = 424.15
  10. Conclusion: the workload holds 85 % of the provisioned volume, option a

Historical Background

Reading a part against a hundred is a merchant habit, not a classroom one. Roman tax collectors and lenders already quoted charges in hundredths of a sum, and Italian trading houses of the fifteenth century wrote the same idea as per cento in their ledgers, where it settled interest, brokerage and losses at sea across contracts of different size.

The reason the habit survived is the reason a cloud team still uses it. A share per hundred lets a manager compare a small volume with a large one, or last quarter with this one, without holding either total in mind.

Named Properties Behind Each Step

The reading above is a chain of inferences, each one licensed by a property of the real numbers. Naming them turns a rule of thumb into an argument a reviewer can audit.

Setup

Let T be the provisioned capacity, fixed at 499.05 GB. Let C be the volume consumed, fixed at 424.15 GB. Let p be the percent of the volume consumed, the unknown.

Notation

  • T is the capacity in GB, 499.05
  • C is the volume consumed in GB, 424.15
  • p is the percent consumed, a positive rational number
  • q is the remainder in Step 6, an integer
  • x, y, c are any real numbers, used to state a property in general form
  • 1 / 100 is the multiplicative inverse of 100
  • ⇒ reads implies, ⇔ reads if and only if, < reads is less than, ≈ reads is approximately equal to

Steps

  1. Definition of percent. x is p percent of y ⇔ x / y = p / 100, for y not zero. So C / T = p / 100.
  2. Multiplication property of equality. x = y ⇒ cx = cy. So 100(C / T) = 100(p / 100).
  3. Multiplicative inverse and identity. For x not zero, (1 / x)x = 1 and 1y = y. So 100(p / 100) = p, and p = 100(C / T).
  4. Substitution. x = y ⇒ x may replace y. So p = 100(424.15 / 499.05).
  5. Cancellation law. For c not zero, (cx) / (cy) = x / y. So 424.15 / 499.05 = 42415 / 49905 = (5)(8483) / (5)(9981) = 8483 / 9981, and p = 848300 / 9981.
  6. Division algorithm. For integers x and y with y greater than zero, x = yz + q with q below y. So 848300 = (9981)(84) + 9896, and p = 84 + 9896 / 9981.
  7. Order preserved under multiplication by a positive number. x < y and c greater than zero ⇒ cx < cy. Since (0.99)(9981) = 9881.19 < 9896 < 9981, then 0.99 < 9896 / 9981 < 1.
  8. Order preserved under addition. x < y ⇒ x + c < y + c. So 84.99 < p < 85, and p ≈ 84.9915.
  9. Flag, no property available. Rounding to the nearest whole percent rests on reporting convention, not on a theorem. Under that convention p reads 85.

Verification

499.05 = 9981 / 20, so (p / 100)T = (8483 / 9981)(9981 / 20) = 8483 / 20 = 424.15. Both sides match the volume consumed.
