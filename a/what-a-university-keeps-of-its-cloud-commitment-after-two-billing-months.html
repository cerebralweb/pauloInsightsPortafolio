WHAT A UNIVERSITY KEEPS OF ITS CLOUD COMMITMENT AFTER TWO BILLING MONTHS

In this article ❧ A prepaid commitment is bought once and spent in stages, so every drawdown changes the base that the next drawdown applies to. Finance teams who track consumption against the original purchase overstate what is left, because a share of the balance is not a share of the pool. This problem shows how two consumption rates applied in sequence settle the closing balance of a budget, and why the order of the draws decides the result.

The Problem

A university bought a prepaid cloud commitment of 240 credits at the start of March. In March the campus workloads consumed 40 percent of the available credits. In April they consumed 25 percent of the credits still left.

How many credits remained in the commitment at the end of April?

  • a) 108
  • b) 120
  • c) 216
  • d) 36
Click to Reveal the Answer

Correct Answer: a) 108 credits ✅

Math Solution

March takes 40 percent of 240, which leaves a balance of 144. April then takes 25 percent of that 144, not of the original 240, so the second draw is 36 credits and the closing balance is 108.

Two successive draws reduce the pool to the product of the surviving fractions, three fifths of three quarters, or nine twentieths of the purchase.

Step-by-Step Breakdown

The following sequence shows how the final amount is derived:

  1. Opening pool: 240 credits
  2. March rate as a fraction: 40 percent = 40/100 = 2/5
  3. March consumption: (40/100)(240) = 9600/100 = 96 credits
  4. Balance after March: 240 − 96 = 144 credits
  5. April rate as a fraction: 25 percent = 25/100 = 1/4
  6. April consumption: (25/100)(144) = 3600/100 = 36 credits
  7. Balance after April: 144 − 36 = 108 credits
  8. Factor check: (240)(3/5)(3/4) = (240)(9/20) = 108 credits
  9. Trap to avoid: 25 percent of 240 gives 60 and a false balance of 84, because the second rate applies to the remainder

Step by Step Proof

The breakdown above gives the arithmetic. This section names the property that licenses each move, so every line rests on a rule that holds outside this problem.

Setup

Let F0 be the credits in the commitment at the start of March, and let C1 and R1 be the credits consumed in March and the credits left at the end of March. Let C2 and R2 be the credits consumed in April and the credits left at the end of April. The purchase fixes F0 = 240. March consumption is 40 percent of the credits available, so C1 = (40/100)F0, and what is left is R1 = F0 − C1. April consumption is 25 percent of the credits still left, so C2 = (25/100)R1, and R2 = R1 − C2.

Notation

  • F0 is the credits available at the start of March, 240.
  • C1 is the credits consumed in March, fixed in Step 2.
  • R1 is the credits left at the end of March, fixed in Step 3.
  • C2 is the credits consumed in April, fixed in Step 5.
  • R2 is the credits left at the end of April, the unknown, a positive rational.
  • q% is a rate per hundred, q being 40 for March and 25 for April.
  • x, y, c, q are any reals, used to state a property in general form.
  • ⇒ reads implies.

Solution

  1. Definition of a percent. Property. Percent. q% = q/100 for a real q. Inference. C1 = (40/100)(240).
  2. Cancellation law. Property. Cancellation law. For y ≠ 0, (yx)/y = x. Inference. C1 = (40)(240)/100 = 9600/100 = (100)(96)/100 = 96. From line 1.
  3. Substitution. Property. Substitution. x = y ⇒ x may replace y. Inference. R1 = 240 − 96 = 144. From line 2.
  4. Definition of a percent, as in Step 1. Property. Percent, as in Step 1. Inference. C2 = (25/100)(144). From line 3.
  5. Cancellation law, as in Step 2. Property. Cancellation law, as in Step 2. Inference. C2 = (25)(144)/100 = 3600/100 = (100)(36)/100 = 36. From line 4.
  6. Substitution, as in Step 3. Property. Substitution, as in Step 3. Inference. R2 = 144 − 36 = 108. From line 5.
  7. Distributivity of multiplication over addition. Property. Distributivity. cx + cy = c(x + y), so x − (q/100)x = x(1 − q/100). Inference. R2 = (240)(1 − 40/100)(1 − 25/100) = (240)(3/5)(3/4) = (240)(9/20) = 108. From lines 1, 3 and 4.

Verification

R2 + C2 = 108 + 36 = 144 = R1, and R1 + C1 = 144 + 96 = 240 = F0. Both constraints match.

Answer

R2 = 108. The commitment held 108 credits at the end of April, which is option a.

Historical Background

Rates per hundred entered accounting through Roman taxation, where the centesima rerum venalium set a levy of one part in a hundred on sales. Italian merchant manuals of the fifteenth century, Pacioli's among them, turned the practice into a method for trade, and their exercises on chained deductions came from a habit of commerce, since a carrier fee, a broker fee and a spoilage allowance each fell on what the previous deduction had spared.

Sequential rates outlived that setting. Trade discounts, depreciation schedules, insurance retentions and cloud commitment drawdowns all rest on the same order of operations, and the dispute they settle is which base a rate attaches to.
